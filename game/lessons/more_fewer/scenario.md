# More, fewer - the night of the fireflies

Goal: compare two amounts WITHOUT counting - give one to each (one-to-one), and what is left without a pair shows
where there are more; "as many" when nothing is left; a long row is not more (the row-length trap); make two
amounts as many by adding, taking away or moving one over. Characters: all four - Owl (the host, a night bird), Fox
(the boaster), Squirrel (afraid of the dark), Deer (the calm one who checks). Stage 3, after counting_3.

**Misconception:** a long row is more. Fox lays three lanterns far apart - "enough for everyone!"; at home it comes
back: Owl's team's two pillows lie in a long row and Fox cries that they have more.

**One thread:** at sunset Owl invites everyone to the glade - tonight the fireflies dance - and to a sleepover at her
home afterwards. The story is the night (the lanterns, the dance, the games), the home stage is the promised
sleepover, ending with everyone asleep and the fireflies dancing outside the window.

## Stage 1 - story

A. *Sunset at Owl's tree* (panorama `polana_wieczorem`). Owl's invitation. Squirrel is afraid of the dark (close-up,
fear bubble). Fox: "we'll take lanterns, one each!" - he brings three, laid out in a LONG row: "enough for everyone!".
On a plain-colour **insert** the trap is drawn: the friends' heads close together, the lanterns far apart; Deer: a long
row is not more - let's check, everyone takes one. **Child** drags the lanterns into the friends' paws, one each; one
friend is left without (close-up, sad). Two-shot: Deer - more friends than lanterns, fewer lanterns; Fox - the long row
fooled me. He runs for one more; now nobody is left out: as many. A pan along the glade as they walk.

B. *The glade at night* (`noc`, the moon). The jars are empty; fireflies drift over the glade. **Child** points to
each lantern - a firefly flies in and it lights up, the dark opens around it (all four, each shown). Squirrel is no
longer afraid. Fireflies are still flying: **child** points - more fireflies or more lanterns? (two cards) - more
fireflies. The free ones fly away over the trees to the big forest clearing where the dance is. Owl has a map: two
paths. Deer: fireflies fly where there are more of them. **Child** leads the lit lantern along the path with MORE
fireflies on Owl's map (`wedrowka` `mapa_swietlikow`, eight fireflies by the left path, two by the right one; the other
path shakes the lantern). Fox: this way! The payoff of the night, seen: the event illustration `taniec_swietlikow`
(the four with their lanterns under the swirling dance).

C. *The breather and the game of stumps.* Squirrel, brave now, proposes hide-and-seek in the dark: Owl looks, the
others hide behind bushes, one calls "a kuku!", **child** points to the bush (`ktoPowiedzial`, non-maths). Then Owl
teaches the game of stumps (two-shot with Fox's catch): while the music plays everyone walks, when it stops everyone
jumps onto a stump. **Child** stops the music each round.
- 4 friends, 3 stumps: Fox is left standing (close-up). **Child**: friends or stumps - which are more? (cards) -
  more friends, fewer stumps (medium shot on the card).
- **Child** adds one stump; music, stop - everyone stands on a stump: as many (two-shot Fox / Deer).
- Owl referees: 3 players, 4 stumps - **child** points to the empty stump - more stumps, fewer players.
A shooting star ends the night: make a wish - and home, to the sleepover.

## Stage 2 - practice (night-blue plain colour with stars, five stars, five different games)

1. **Without a pair**: bunnies joined to carrots by pencil lines but one - point to the bunny with no pair
   ("Tak, ten zajączek nie ma pary! Zajączków jest więcej niż marchewek.").
2. **The long row**: three apples spread far, four pears close - which are more? A wrong pick draws the pairing.
   Named: more pears, one has no pair.
3. **One each** (`rozdaj`, `ile: 1`): five carrots, three bunnies - one to each; two are left: more carrots.
4. **Too many balloons**: four balloons, two bunnies; pop balloons until each bunny has one: as many.
5. **A quick look**: two small groups of mice run behind two bushes one by one; behind which are more? They come
   out again and the answer is named.
Each answer is named with the answer in a medium shot; Owl's praise in a close-up at the end.

## Stage 3 - at home: the third act (`pokoj_noc`, Owl's room at night)

1. **Arrival with the thread**: after the night of the fireflies, the promised sleepover - "let's have a pillow
   fight!". Fox: two teams.
2. **The misconception again**: Fox's team has four pillows close together, Owl's team two in a long row; Fox (close-up,
   angry): your row is longer - you have more, not fair!
3. **Interactions, all different**:
   - on a plain-colour **insert** (the method): Deer - a long row is not more, let's check in pairs; Owl joins the
     pillows team to team with nested arcs; **child** points to a pillow without a pair (`task`) - two of Fox's: his
     team has more; Fox: the long row fooled me again;
   - **child** drags ONE pillow to the other team (free drag); paired again - as many, fair;
   - back in the room the pillow fight (feathers); bedtime: **child** lays one pillow for each of us on the painted rug
     (`miejsca` dywan, four places, `slots`) - two are left over: more pillows than us;
   - the fireflies want to go home: **child** points to each lantern and its firefly flies out through the window.
4. **The payoff SEEN**: the event illustration `nocowanie` - all four asleep on the rug, each on its own pillow, the
   fireflies dancing by the moon outside. Owl: good night.
5. **The home task shown in play**: the pillows laid one each was it; Squirrel to the parents: when you share, lay
   things in pairs - if nothing is left without a pair, everyone has as many.
