# More, fewer - PRO: the night of the fireflies (design, awaiting the tools' approval)

Goal: compare two amounts WITHOUT counting - give one to each (one-to-one), and what is left without a pair shows
where there are more; "as many" when nothing is left; a long row is not more (the row-length trap); make two
amounts as many by adding, taking away or moving one over. Characters: all four for the first time - Owl (the
host, a night bird), Fox (the boaster), Squirrel (afraid of the dark), Deer (the calm one who checks).

The thread: at sunset Owl invites everyone to the glade - tonight the fireflies dance - and to a sleepover at
home afterwards. The night is the stage: it gets dark, and every light the child makes appears in the dark
(tool proposal 1).

## Stage 1 - story

1. **Sunset, before the walk.** Owl's invitation. Squirrel is afraid of the dark (a fear bubble): "it will be
   dark on the glade". Fox: "we'll take lanterns, one each!" - he brings three, laid out in a LONG row: "look how
   many - enough for everyone!" (the four friends stand close together: their row is shorter). Deer: a long row is
   not yet more - let's check: everyone takes one. The child drags the lanterns to the friends, one each; Squirrel
   is left without one (her fear again). On a plain colour the method is drawn: the friends' heads in a row, the
   lanterns under them, a pencil line from each lantern to one head - one head has no line: there are more friends
   than lanterns, fewer lanterns. Fox runs back for one more; now nobody is left out: as many. Off they go (a pan,
   the sun setting).
2. **The glade at night.** Dark (tool 1). The lanterns are empty glass jars; fireflies drift over the glade. Owl:
   a firefly comes to a lantern that calls it - tap a lantern. Each tapped lantern takes in one firefly and lights
   up, the dark opens around it. All four lit - and fireflies are still flying: are there more fireflies or more
   lanterns? (two cards: a lantern, a firefly) - more fireflies, they stay free and dance. Squirrel is no longer
   afraid.
3. **The game of stumps.** Owl teaches it first (guidelines: a game is taught before it is played): while the
   music plays we walk around; when it stops everyone jumps onto a stump; whoever has none is out. The child is
   the one who stops the music (a big button, tool 2).
   - 4 friends, 3 stumps: Fox is left standing. Friends or stumps - which are more? (cards with their heads and a
     stump).
   - "So that nobody is left out": the child drags one more stump into the ring; music, stop - everyone stands on
     a stump: as many.
   - Owl stays out to be the referee: 3 friends, 4 stumps - one stump stays empty: point to it - more stumps.
   A shooting star ends the night: make a wish - and home, to the sleepover.

## Stage 2 - practice - inserts on plain colours (night blue, stars), five stars, five different games

1. **Without a pair**: two rows already joined by pencil lines but one - tap the one without a pair.
2. **The long row**: a long spread row and a short tight one (the long one has fewer); where are more? A wrong
   pick draws the pairing, which shows it.
3. **Make it as many**: three bunnies, one carrot; drag carrots from the basket until every bunny has one.
4. **Too many balloons**: four balloons, two children; pop balloons (tap) until each child has one.
5. **A quick look**: two small groups flash up and hide; where were there more?

## Stage 3 - at home - the children's room, the sleepover

A pillow fight, two teams (Fox and Squirrel, Owl and Deer). Fox's team has more pillows - not fair! The pairing is
drawn across (team to team), two are left over. "Fair is as many": the child moves ONE pillow to the other team;
paired again - as many. The pillow fight (feathers fly), then bedtime under a night light. Owl gives the sticker
(a lit lantern). "When you share something with someone, lay the things in pairs: that's how you check everyone
has as many."
