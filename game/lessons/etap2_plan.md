# Stage 2 - the plan (tall and short, in order, shapes, morning and night)

The four lessons of the sun stage, planned like stage 1 (guidelines.md: the story leads, one thread over the three
stages, a naive try with a visible consequence, a helping adventure, a breather without maths, five different
practice games, the home game; about 10 minutes). The new cast gets its first parts here: each lesson brings in one
of them, so the stage never repeats its faces.

## 5. Tall and short (new)

**Idea:** tall / short (height, standing up - not size, not length lying down), taller / shorter, the tallest /
the shortest; the method: stand them on one ground line (lesson 4's start line, now upright).
**Misconception:** the one standing higher up looks taller (a short one on a stump vs a tall one on the grass).

- **Story.** Bear wants to see the forest from the treehouse, but the ladder is broken.
  1. *Forest.* Bear (the tallest of all) meets Squirrel and Fox; who can reach the lowest branch? Squirrel stands
     on a stump: "I'm the tallest now!" (naive try) - she jumps and does not reach. Fox: stand on the same ground
     to compare - **child** points to the tallest when all stand on the grass (Bear), then the shortest.
  2. Bear reaches the branch, but the ladder's rungs are gone; sticks lie about - **child** picks the tallest
     stick for the ladder's side (overlap with the line at the foot: `odLinii`, upright).
  3. *Orchard, helping.* Mother Hedgehog's apples hang on trees: which tree is the shortest - her little one can
     reach that one (point to it).
  4. *Breather:* a game of "grow and shrink" - when Bear says "tall!" everybody stretches up, "short!" crouches;
     the child points to whoever got it wrong (statues mechanics).
  5. From the treehouse Bear sees far - and invites them home.
- **Practice:** which is taller (two trees, two giraffe-like sunflowers); the tallest of three towers (`wieza`
  built already); the shortest candle; stand them on the line (a stick that floats higher is pulled down by the
  child: drag to the line); shadows of tall and short.
- **Home (hallway):** a height chart on the wall - the friends stand by it: point to the tallest, the shortest;
  "measure yourself by the door frame with your parents".

## 6. In order (rethought)

**Why rethink:** the old lesson is three unconnected rows (cups, carrots, blocks) plus five rows in practice -
the same drag-into-places eight times, and cups that "do not fit" without showing why. **New:** one thread with a
consequence: Beaver builds a tower for the river's lookout and it only stands when the blocks go biggest to
smallest (`wieza` - a wrong order tips and falls, the mechanic made for this).
**Misconception:** "put them in any order, a tower is a tower" - Bunny in a hurry stacks small on big, it falls.

- **Story.** Beaver's lookout tower on the brook.
  1. *Brook.* Beaver measures with his tail ("I'll measure it with my tail!"), Bunny wants to help and stacks in a
     hurry - the tower falls (`wieza`, naive try). Beaver: in order - from the biggest at the bottom to the smallest
     on top. **Child** builds the tower (`wieza`, four blocks).
  2. The steps down to the water: stones from the shortest to the tallest (seriation into places, `slots`).
  3. *Garden, helping.* Owl's nesting boxes got mixed: the three owlets sit by size - the biggest in the biggest box
     (drag to places).
  4. *Breather:* a race on the number line without numbers (`os` with no digits): Bunny hops, Beaver walks -
     cheer who comes first (who is first, who is last - just watched).
  5. From the tower they see Owl's house - off home.
- **Practice:** five different games - stack cups inside each other (in order); carrots from the shortest; the
  missing one in a growing row (`slots` with one gap); which row is in order (pointing between two rows); a tower.
- **Home (room):** toys on the shelf from the biggest to the smallest; "line up the family's shoes".

## 7. Shapes (rethought)

**Why rethink:** good content (corners and sides, a turned square), but told as a lecture with Owl's birdhouse
and quiz rounds in practice. **New:** a house for the shy Hedgehog built from shapes (`ksztalt`, `tangram`,
`place`), the shapes found by tracing with a finger (`slad`), the corners counted by touch.
**Misconception:** a turned square is "not a square any more"; colour tells the shape.

- **Story.** Hedgehog has no house for the winter; friends build one from shapes.
  1. *Meadow.* Hedgehog, shy, hides; Fox and Owl promise a house. Shapes lie in the grass: Owl traces a circle with
     her wing - the **child** traces the circle, the triangle, the square (`slad`) and counts corners (`count`).
  2. Building: walls (square), roof (triangle), window (circle), door (rectangle) - **child** drags each to its
     outline (`place` / `slots` with accepts by kind). A turned square still fits.
  3. *Playground, helping.* Bunny's kite lost its shape - which shape is it (a turned square), point to it.
  4. *Breather:* a shadow game - whose shadow is it (`cien`) among shapes.
  5. The house is done; Hedgehog comes out, happy.
- **Practice:** tangram (`tangram` domek); point to the triangle among turned shapes; trace a shape (`slad`);
  memory of shapes (`memory`); colour by number (`kolorowanka`, the house).
- **Home (kitchen):** what shape is a plate, a sandwich, a window; "look for shapes at home with your parents".

## 8. Morning and night (new)

**Idea:** the parts of the day in order - morning, day, evening, night - and what we do in each; before / after.
**Misconception:** Mole, who lives underground and sees badly, thinks it is always night.

- **Story.** Mole wakes up in his tunnel: is it morning? He cannot tell.
  1. *Tunnel → meadow.* Deer and Squirrel take him up: the sun rises (`noc` fades out, light). They show him
     morning things (breakfast, washing). **Child** points to what we do in the morning.
  2. The day passes on the meadow (the light changes: `noc` with strengths; a sun moving): **child** puts the four
     pictures of the day in order (`slots`: morning, midday, evening, night).
  3. *Helping.* Squirrel's little brother is lost at dusk: lanterns (`swiatlo`) in the evening - find him.
  4. *Breather:* catch the fireflies at night.
  5. Night: Mole: now I know - after evening comes night, and after night the morning again.
- **Practice:** what comes next (morning → ?); what we do at night (sleep) vs in the day; order three pictures;
  memory day/night pairs; what is wrong in this picture (the moon at breakfast - `roznice`).
- **Home (room, `pokoj_noc` and `pokoj`):** the evening routine in order - supper, bath, pyjamas, story, sleep;
  "tell your parents what you did this morning, this afternoon and this evening".
