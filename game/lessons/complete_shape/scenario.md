# The missing piece

Goal: a gap has a shape, and the piece that fits it has the same shape (and the same turn) - not the same colour; a
whole picture can be put together from pieces. Age 3-4. Characters: Owl (left, the painter), Squirrel (right, the hasty
one), Hedgehog (right, the birthday boy). Stage 6, the last lesson of world 1: the child knows the shapes (circle,
square, triangle, rectangle), the tangram (a tap turns a piece), counting to 5, the comparisons.

**Misconception:** colour decides - Squirrel: "the hole is in the red roof, so the red piece fits" (a red square).

One thread: **Owl's picture for Hedgehog's birthday** - the wind tears it, the friends put it together, find the
missing piece by its shape, colour a frame for it and mend the footbridge to Hedgehog's; the picture is given at the
party, and at Hedgehog's birthday tea (home) a plate breaks, Squirrel repeats her mistake, the gift is a shape puzzle,
the card lacks a string, and the picture finally stands on the windowsill.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. Owl's oak (`dziupla_sowy`) - the picture is ready, the wind takes it
1. Owl and Squirrel under the oak (two-shot). Owl: "I've finished! I painted a picture for Hedgehog's birthday."
   Squirrel: "Show me, show me!"
2. The picture flies up from Owl (found picture `obrazek_jeza`): a cottage with a red triangular roof, the sun, a little
   hedgehog by the door. Squirrel: who did Owl paint? **Child** points to Hedgehog on the picture. Owl names it: yes,
   Hedgehog in front of his house - it's his birthday today.
3. A gust (leaves blow across, a whoosh): the picture shakes, tumbles up and away out of the frame. Owl (close-up, "!"):
   "Oh no! The wind took my picture!" Squirrel (two-shot): it tore into pieces - they flew to the meadow! Owl: let's
   find them, quickly.

### B. The meadow (`laka`) - put it together, the missing piece, the frame
4. Arrival (wide): Squirrel - here they are, all the pieces in the grass. Owl: put the picture back together, every
   piece has its place. **Child** puts the torn picture together (`podarty`, 4 pieces, first use).
5. The picture is whole - but for a triangular hole in the roof (medium on Owl). Owl (sad): almost... one piece is
   missing! Squirrel: three more pieces lie in the grass - a red square, a green circle, a triangle (red and blue) -
   they come up under the picture.
6. Squirrel's mistake (medium on Squirrel and the pieces): "The hole is in the red roof, so the red piece fits!" The red
   square flies to the hole: its corners stick out, the hole's corners still show; it wobbles and goes back. Squirrel
   (close-up): "It doesn't fit, it sticks out!" Owl: colour isn't enough - look at the edges of the hole.
7. **Child** points to the piece with the same shape as the hole (wide: picture and pieces). The triangle flies in and
   the picture is whole. Owl names it: a triangle - the hole has three corners and so has this piece. Squirrel: the
   picture is whole!
8. **Insert (plain colour, drifting shapes):** a dark triangular hole alone; a red square laid on it - corners stick out,
   the hole peeps out; a blue triangle laid on it - it covers it exactly. Owl: a piece fits when it has the same shape
   as the hole. Squirrel (close-up): "Aha! I don't look at the colour, I look at the shape."
9. *Breather, no maths* (`malowanie` on the scratch-off pair `rama_obrazka`, back on the meadow): Squirrel - Hedgehog
   will get it in a frame! **Child** colours the frame with a finger (the colours come out under it). Owl admires it;
   the framed picture (sticker) is ready. Owl: Hedgehog lives across the brook - let's go.

### C. The brook (`strumyk`) - the footbridge, the party
10. Owl and Squirrel walk in; Hedgehog comes from the other side to meet them (two-shot): "Hi! Are you coming to my
    birthday?" Owl: yes, and we have a surprise. Hedgehog (close-up, worried): oh no - the little plank bridge over the
    brook has a hole, we can't get across!
11. The footbridge (pieces laid across the water like coloured planks - a tangram silhouette, one square missing).
    **Child** points to the plank that plugs the hole (square / triangle / parallelogram). The square slides in. Owl
    names it: the hole is square, so the square fits. Hedgehog: hooray, come to my house!
12. **Event illustration** `urodziny_jeza`: Hedgehog holds up the picture in its colourful frame at his party. Owl:
    happy birthday, this is for you. Hedgehog: my house and me! Thank you - come to my birthday tea! (the thread home)

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert on soft lilac, drifting shapes, five stars) - "the piece school"
1. **Which piece fits the gap:** a fish laid from pieces with its tail missing; the two wrong pieces have the colour of
   the fish's body, the right one (a triangle) another colour - **child** points to the one of the right shape.
2. **Turn the piece:** a boat to lay from two pieces that lie turned the wrong way - a tap turns them.
3. **A torn picture of more pieces:** Owl's second painting (`obrazek_staw`, a pond) torn into six pieces - put it
   together (no number said: no counting past five).
4. **What is missing:** a mug without its handle; **child** points to what it is missing - the handle, a spoon or a
   saucer; the handle flies onto the mug.
5. **A picture from three pieces:** a fox's head from one big and two small triangles (tangram).

## Stage 3 - at home - the third act (Hedgehog's room, `pokoj`, its windowsill as a drop place)
13. Arrival with the thread (two-shot Hedgehog / Owl): welcome to my room - time for the birthday tea.
14. The home problem: Squirrel carries the blue plate - it slips, falls and breaks (the plate with a wedge-shaped gap,
    three pieces around: the wedge, a blue square shard, a yellow round shard). Squirrel (close-up, scared): oh no!
    Hedgehog: never mind, we'll mend it. Squirrel (the misconception again): "The plate is blue, so the blue piece
    fits!" - the blue square goes to the gap and sticks out. Owl (close-up, "?"): blue - but square! Remember the
    picture: we look at the shape. **Child** points to the piece that fits the gap; the wedge slides in; Hedgehog names
    it.
15. Squirrel's gift lies under wrapping paper: **child** rubs it off (scratch-off `prezent_jeza`) - a box of wooden
    shape pieces. Squirrel: **child** points to the triangle in the box. Hedgehog names it.
16. Hedgehog: let's build a house like the one on my picture - **child** lays it from the new pieces on the floor
    (tangram: two squares and a triangle roof). Owl names it.
17. Owl's birthday card (found picture `kartka_urodzinowa`): something is missing - **child** points to the balloon
    without a string; Owl draws the string in pencil: now the balloon is whole.
18. The payoff: Hedgehog wants Owl's picture where everyone sees it - **child** stands the framed picture on the
    windowsill (`miejsca`: `parapet`); everyone claps. Hedgehog (close-up): the best birthday ever, thank you.
19. The home game shown (insert): a picture cut into four lies in pieces and the friends put it together on screen.
    Owl (close-up) to the parents: cut a picture into four pieces and put it together with your parents.

## Iterations
- v1 (2026-10-30), from the plan in etap6_plan.md ("New lessons", 24). Changes from the plan: the torn picture's hole
  is painted half in the red roof and half in the blue sky, so the right piece is a red-and-blue triangle and the red
  square is the right colour but the wrong shape (one right shape, one right colour, one wrong both: a green circle);
  the story's first beat gets a small task (point to Hedgehog on the picture) so the child helps before the idea; the
  breather colours the frame through `malowanie` on a scratch-off pair (the plain frame wiped into a colourful one) -
  `malowanie` has no "colour this" mode of its own; the bridge's gap is a footbridge laid from tangram pieces over the
  brook (`strumyk`), and Hedgehog meets the friends there; the picture goes onto the WINDOWSILL, not the wall - `pokoj`
  has no wall place among its `miejsca`; the home scratch-off's gift (a box of shape pieces) is what the house is then
  built from, and the home game for the parents is shown with the torn-picture tool putting itself together.
