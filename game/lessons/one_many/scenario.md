# One or many?

Goal: one thing / many things ("jeden" / "wiele"): see at a glance whether there is one of something or many, and
say it. Not yet numbers. Age 3-4. Characters: Squirrel (left), Fox (left), Deer (right). Stage 1, lesson 1.

One thread: **winter is coming and Squirrel gathers her store** - the story fills it, the practice is "checking the
store", at home the store becomes a feast.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. The garden - big is not many (the concept comes in)
1. Squirrel and Deer in the garden, autumn leaves in the air. Squirrel: winter is coming, I need a store - lots
   of food, many nuts! Fox comes in rolling one enormous pumpkin: "I brought you a lot of food!" (the naive try:
   big means many).
2. Deer: Fox, that is ONE pumpkin - big, but one. Squirrel: then what is "many"? Deer: many is when there are
   lots of things - this one, and this, and this... and shows a heap of nuts (each nut bumps as she says "and
   this"). The concept named: one - many.
3. **Child** points: where is one thing? (the pumpkin) Where are many? (the nuts).
4. The garden's harvest lies in a row over their heads: one pear, many apples, one mushroom, many acorns,
   one carrot, many leaves. **Child** drags each onto the plate ("one") or into the basket ("many").
   Wrong: it comes back; Deer: "is it one thing, or many?"
5. Fox, a little ashamed, laughs: then the pumpkin goes to the store too - one big pumpkin and many nuts!

### B. The brook - helping Mother Duck
6. Off to the brook for water. Mother Duck is upset (a sweat bubble): her many ducklings swam off, one is missing.
   The ducklings come back together in a group - many - but one is missing.
7. **Child** finds the one duckling, alone, hidden at the side among the reeds (a pointing task among bushes and
   the lone duckling). It swims back to the others; Mother Duck is happy (a heart).
8. *Breather, no maths:* the ducklings play hide and seek - they pop up here and there for a moment; **child**
   catches three (a quick catching game).
9. Squirrel: thank you, ducks! Now home with the store - and a feast for everybody!

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert, autumn leaves drifting, five stars) - "checking the store"
1. **Listen:** Fox claps once or many times; **child** points to the card - one clap / many claps (`dzwieki`).
2. **Cups:** one acorn under one cup, many under another; the cups mix (`kubki`); where are many?
3. **Memory:** pairs one apple-one apple, many apples-many apples, one nut-one nut, many nuts-many nuts (`memory`).
4. **Shadows:** whose shadow is it - one pear or many pears? (`cien`).
5. **One among many:** many stars and one moon - point to what there is one of.

## Stage 3 - at home - the feast (the kitchen)
10. The store is home; a feast for the friends. Squirrel: one cake for all of us - **child** drags the one cake to
    the plate on the table; and many cherries - **child** drags many cherries into the bowl (all of them).
11. Deer: one cake and many cherries! Fox: ask your parents to look around with you - what do you have one of at
    home, and what many of?

## Iterations
- v1 was a sorting lesson (sort pictures into one/many) only. Critique: sorting alone is the old scheme, and "many"
  pictures are abstract. v2 gives it the store thread and the naive try (one big pumpkin - "a lot of food"), which
  is the real confusion of a 3-year-old (big ~ many).
- The duckling is the helping adventure; "one missing from many" makes the child look for the one apart.
- Watched as a child: every line is said by someone on screen; the heap of nuts is counted "and this, and this"
  while each nut bumps, so "many" is seen and heard; the one duckling is clearly apart from the group, not inside it.
