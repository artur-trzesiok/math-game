# Lesson 1: Bigger, smaller

Goal: the child compares two (then three) things by size and says which is bigger /
smaller, biggest / smallest. No numbers, no measuring - the eye decides, the word
names it. Characters: Fox (left) and Squirrel (right).

## Stage 1 - story (orchard)

1. Both friends walk in. Narrator: they found two apples. A big apple appears by
   Fox, a small one by Squirrel. Fox points at his: "my apple is big"; Squirrel: "mine
   is small, but sweet". Narrator names the relation both ways (bigger than / smaller
   than) while each apple pulses in turn. Everyday bridge: mum's shoes and the child's.
2. Squirrel is thirsty: two cups, big and small. Fox: "Squirrel has little paws, help
   her choose". The child taps the smaller cup (a wrong tap explains and asks again;
   after two misses the right one pulses). The cup slides to Squirrel, she cheers,
   narrator sums up.

## Stage 2 - practice (orchard, Fox alone)

- Five pairs (pear, ball, cookie, flower, sock) at two sizes, left/right randomised;
  Fox asks for the bigger or the smaller one, alternating. Right answer: "that's
  right" + applause; wrong: "try again", hint after two misses.
- Two triples (apples, cups) at three sizes: "tap the biggest", "tap the smallest".

## Stage 3 - apply (kitchen)

Fox and Squirrel each give a real-life mission (a spoon bigger than your hand,
something smaller than your thumb), then a card for the parent: line up the
family's shoes from smallest to biggest and ask which are bigger / smallest.

## Notes for the recording

Lines are short and literal; "bigger / smaller" are always said as a pair at least
once so the child hears both. Fox is lively, Squirrel is soft, the narrator calm.
