# Bigger and smaller - a new home for Squirrel

Goal: bigger / smaller, the biggest / the smallest - the hollows compared with the old hollow's round door held against each new hole (a hollow cannot be carried, its door can); small things laid one on the other (the bigger one sticks
out all round). Age 3-4. Characters: Squirrel and Fox (left), Owl (right). Stage 1, lesson 3 - it follows "one or
many?": the winter store made her hollow too small.

One thread: **Squirrel has outgrown her hollow** - the story finds the just-right one and moves the store in, the
practice is "playing big and small before the party", at home the housewarming in the new hollow, where every box,
chair and cup must be the right size.

## Why rethought
The first version was four unconnected things (rain and wellies, rock-paper-scissors at the brook, Owl's hat, a present
of hats at home - hats twice), and it compared things handed to the characters rather than a problem they had. Now one
thread with a visible consequence at every step: too small - too big - just right.

## Stage 1 - story (the forest)
A. Squirrel and her old hollow: the store is ready. She squeezes in - **event illustration: Squirrel stuck in the old
hollow, only her head in** (the problem, big and funny); she pops out (sweat). Fox: it is too small, you have grown and
your store is big. Owl: you need a bigger one. Squirrel: how will we know which one is bigger? **Insert on a plain
colour (the method):** the old hollow and its round door that fits exactly - a hollow cannot be carried, its door can;
hold it against each new hole: a gap all round - bigger; the door covering it and sticking out - smaller.
B. Three hollows. **Child** holds the door against each hole in any order (Fox says what it shows each time), then
points to the biggest - Squirrel tries the biggest (the misconception: the biggest must be best): too big, the wind
blows, cold. **Child** points to the smallest - too small. **Child** points to the one just right - a little bigger than
the old one. Helping: the mice moving into the old hollow need a bigger box; **child** points to it. Moving day - who
carries the first box? *Breather, no maths:* rock, paper, scissors against Fox (taught first).
C. *Squirrel's new oak* (`dab_wiewiorki`, a place of its own: the just-right hollow halfway up a big oak full of
acorns). Arrival: Squirrel shows her new home - not too big, not too small; Owl: so many acorns. The store is in two
boxes, a big and a small one; the winner of the game carries the first box up into the hollow (**child** drags a box
into it, Fox throws the other); all the store fits,
because this hollow is bigger than the old one. Squirrel invites everybody to a housewarming.

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert, five stars) - "big and small before the party"
Which is bigger (twice), then the overlap shows it; the biggest of three apples (named: "yes, the biggest apple"); the
shadow of the big or the small pear; caps by size onto Fox, Owl and Squirrel (a wrong one slips off or does not go on),
named at the end ("the biggest on Owl's big head, the smallest on mine").

## Stage 3 - at home - the housewarming (inside the new hollow, `dziupla_wnetrze`, painted places)
1. Arrival with the thread: the friends come to the housewarming; first the store goes onto the shelves - one big
   shelf, one small one (`miejsca`: `polka_duza`, `polka_mala`).
2. The home problem with the misconception: Squirrel puts the BIG box of the store on the little shelf "because it is
   near the window" - it sticks out on both sides, wobbles and falls (sweat). Owl: the box is bigger than the shelf - a
   big box on the big shelf, a small one on the small shelf. **Child** puts both boxes where they fit.
3. The chairs: three chairs at three sizes; **child** points to the one for Squirrel - the smallest.
4. The tea: **child** gives the big cup to big Fox and the small cup to little Squirrel (dragged to them).
5. The payoff, seen: **event illustration** - the housewarming party, every box, chair and cup its own size.
6. The home task shown in play, on a plain-colour insert: two shoes, a big and a small one; **child** points to the
   bigger, then Fox lays one on the other - the bigger sticks out all round. Owl: compare the family's shoes with your
   parents the same way.

## Iterations
- v1 was four unconnected things; v2 one thread (too small - too big - just right) with the old door as the template.
- v3 (2026-10-29, the improvement plan): the problem became an event illustration (Squirrel stuck in the old hollow,
  only her head in); the method is shown on a plain-colour insert; the catching game became rock-paper-scissors for who
  carries the first box (then the box really goes into the new hollow); practice 3 names the biggest apple and the caps end with a named line; the home
  stage became a third act in Squirrel's new hollow (painted shelves as drop places): Squirrel puts the big box on the
  small shelf, the chairs, the cups, the housewarming seen as an illustration, the shoe comparison played on screen
  before it is given to the parents; every "Wskaż." became one sentence; the camera edited shot by shot.
