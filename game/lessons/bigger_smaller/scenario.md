# Lesson 1: Bigger, smaller

Goal: the child compares two (then three) things by size and says which is bigger /
smaller, biggest / smallest. No numbers, no measuring - the eye decides, the word
names it. Characters: Fox (left) and Squirrel (right).

## Stage 1 - story - a journey: orchard, forest, kitchen (six choices)

1. Orchard, the problem: a quarrel. Fox and Squirrel each say their pear is
   bigger; Fox's (the smaller one) hangs higher - "mine is higher, so it is
   bigger!" Squirrel: higher is not bigger (on a stump you are higher, not
   bigger). How then? By comparing: a pencil line, both pears evenly on it, a line
   over each top. The child points to the higher line, then to the bigger pear;
   Fox admits it - comparing settled the quarrel. The rule.
1a. Orchard: a gust, two apples drop; the child points to the BIGGER one for Fox.
   "Let's go to the forest!"
2. Forest. Two pine cones, by eye now (as Fox promised in the orchard): the BIGGER
   one for Squirrel's winter store. A leaf comes
   spinning down from the crowns; two leaves: the SMALLER one fits behind
   Squirrel's ear. "It's getting cold, let's go home!"
3. Kitchen. Two cups: the SMALLER for Squirrel's paws. Two cookies: the BIGGER for
   hungry Fox. Two spoons: the SMALLER for Squirrel's little mouth. More than two
   cups: Fox shows the biggest, Squirrel the smallest, the child points to the
   biggest (practice asks for them). Summary: bigger for the bigger, smaller for the
   smaller.
Every pick is the same thing at two sizes; a wrong pick is named and asked again,
after two misses the right one pulses.

## Stage 2 - practice (orchard, Fox alone)

- Five pairs (pear, ball, cookie, flower, sock) at two sizes, left/right randomised;
  Fox asks for the bigger or the smaller one, alternating. Right answer: "that's
  right" + applause; wrong: "try again", hint after two misses.
- Two triples (apples, cups) at three sizes: "point to the biggest", "point to the smallest".

## Stage 3 - apply (kitchen)

Fox and Squirrel play the home game on screen: "bigger or smaller than my paw?"
(a plate: bigger; a cookie: smaller), then the child plays five rounds (the same
thing at two sizes, "which one is bigger / smaller?"). They end by asking the child to ask
the parents to play it at home. No card, no text.

## Notes for the recording

Lines are short and literal; "bigger / smaller" are always said as a pair at least
once so the child hears both. Fox is lively, Squirrel is soft; there is no narrator.
