# Grouping - PRO: the mystery of the red gifts

Built on guidelines.md "Atmosphere and editing". Goal: group things by a named rule, and see that the same
things can be grouped by different rules - and that the right rule explains something (a detective's tool).
Characters: Squirrel (left), Deer (right), Fox (the shy one, at his den in the forest). One walk across the
panorama "jesienny_sad" (orchard - meadow - forest edge); autumn leaves in the air.

## Stage 1 - story - the mystery

The thread: every morning someone leaves gifts under the apple tree. Deer: when we find who, we invite them home
for an apple pie (paid out in stage 3).

1. **The orchard.** Gifts again under the apple tree, each with a golden bow (named one by one). We must find out
   who it is: be detectives, check what the gifts have in common - maybe it helps us guess who left them. A gust
   blows other things in among them (the gifts keep their bows). The method in Deer's thought bubble:
   grouping = things that belong together go together (an idea bubble). Rule one: food into the bowl, the rest
   into the box - the gifts end up in BOTH groups: not this rule. Rule two: red into the basket, the rest into the
   box - every gift is in the basket: they are all red! A clue: someone loves red. A trail of red leaves...
2. **The meadow.** A cold wind: what is useful against it (a hat and a scarf among a ball and a spoon), dragged
   onto Squirrel; Deer has her fur (quadrupeds are never dressed). Then the trail, a quick game: countdown, and four
   times red and yellow leaves pop up - catch the red one; every catch is a few steps (a short pan) along the
   trail. It leads to the forest - but the forest is big: which way now? The wind blows in a map of the forest (a
   found picture, `znalezisko` "mapa_lasu"): three paths, each strewn with leaves of one colour. The giver loves red,
   so we look for the path of red leaves: the **child** points to the place at its end - the big oak with a little
   door. Off we go (a pan to the forest edge).
3. **The forest edge.** "Look, what a beautiful red oak!" - "Somebody lives here..." - "Oh! And it has a red door.
   This must be the place!" Knock-knock: Fox comes out - it was him, he loves red, he wanted
   to be friends but was shy. Invited for the pie - but he cannot find his red hat in the mess. "Come inside."
4. **Inside Fox's home in the oak** (new scenery `lisia_nora`). "How cosy! Red curtains, a red armchair, a red
   rug..." - "I told you I love red! And here are my hats and scarves, all mixed up" (lying anyhow, turned). Sort his
   things, hats into the basket, scarves into the box; point to the red hat; Fox puts it on. Off home for the pie.

## Stage 2 - practice - inserts on plain colours, five stars

A detective's training, five different mini-games: what fits this group (three red things in Deer's bubble - which
of two joins them); red and yellow leaves into two containers; catch the mushrooms popping up; the odd one out
(three acorns and a chestnut); acorns and chestnuts into two containers.

## Stage 3 - at home - the children's room, with Fox

Fox's first visit; the pie is still baking. The room is a mess: things lie anyhow on the rug. Fox "tidies" -
everything into one box. Deer wants to play ball while the pie bakes - where is it? Somewhere in the jumble: Fox
rummages and cannot find it. Squirrel: group them - toys into the basket, clothes into the box; then finding is
easy. The child sorts; "where should I look for the ball?" - the toys' basket - Fox finds it at once ("grouping is
great!"). The pie is ready (baked while they tidied). "Tidy your room with your parents like this: toys in one
box, clothes in another - you'll see how easy it is to find everything."
