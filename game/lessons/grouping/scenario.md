# Lesson 3: Grouping by a rule

Goal: the child sorts things by a criterion that is said out loud (kind, what it is
for, colour, size) and sees that the same things can be grouped by a different
rule. Characters: Squirrel (left) and Deer (right).

## Stage 1 - story (children's room, after a snack)

1. Apples and pears lie mixed on the rug. Deer: apples to one basket, pears to
   the other. Two outlined zones appear. "Tap all the apples" - each tapped apple
   flies into its zone; a wrong tap is named ("that is not an apple"); after two
   misses the remaining right ones pulse. Then the pears. Everyday bridge: spoons,
   socks, blocks.
2. Squirrel: "can we sort them another way?" The same fruit at two sizes, the rule
   is now big / small. Summary: the same things, different rules - say the rule
   first, then sort.

## Stage 2 - practice (Deer asks, Squirrel cheers)

Four rounds, six things each, one zone: all the cups (kind); everything you can
eat (purpose); everything red (colour: apple, ball); all the small things (size).

## Stage 3 - apply (kitchen)

Squirrel: sort the socks after the laundry, dark with dark. Deer: cars with cars,
blocks with blocks. Card for the parent: name the rule aloud while tidying, then
change the rule.

## Notes

Colour rounds use only objects whose colour is unambiguous in the icons (red
apple, red-and-white ball). Zones are the engine's dashed outlines; items snap
into a 3-column grid inside them.
