# Lesson 3: Grouping by a rule

Goal: the child sorts things by a criterion that is said out loud (kind, what it is
for, colour, size) and sees that the same things can be grouped by a different
rule. Characters: Squirrel (left) and Deer (right).

## Stage 1 - story - a journey: children's room, brook, kitchen (seven interactions)

1. Children's room. Apples and pears mixed on the rug. Action: a pear rolls under
   the bed, the child drags it back onto the rug. Squirrel: apples to one
   container, pears to the other; the child drags everything into a basket and a
   box (any order, checked when all is in), then points to the container with the
   apples.
2. The brook. Pebbles and leaves on the bank: the child sorts them (pebbles /
   leaves). Action: Squirrel throws a pebble into the water, splash! Among the
   pebbles left on the bank one thing is not a pebble: the child points to the
   pine cone.
3. Kitchen. The same fruit, another rule: big into the basket, small into the box;
   then the child points to the container with the big ones. Summary: the same
   things, different rules - say the rule first, then sort.

## Stage 2 - practice (Deer asks, Squirrel cheers)

Four rounds, six things each, basket + box: cups vs the rest (kind); things you
can eat vs the rest (purpose); red things vs the rest (colour: apple, ball);
small vs big (size). Same free dragging, same check.

## Stage 3 - apply (kitchen)

Tidying up after a snack in the kitchen, five rules in a row (food / dishes, cups
/ plates, red / the rest, big / small, round / the rest), basket and box, the same
free dragging; they end by asking the child to ask the parents to tidy like this
at home and then change the rule. No card, no text.

## Notes

Colour rounds use only objects whose colour is unambiguous in the icons (red
apple, red-and-white ball). Containers are engine zones drawn over the basket
and box stickers; items inside are laid out in rows by their real widths, a
little smaller than loose, so the big ones fit too.
