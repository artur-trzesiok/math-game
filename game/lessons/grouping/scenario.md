# Lesson 3: Grouping by a rule

Goal: the child sorts things by a criterion that is said out loud (kind, what it is
for, colour, size) and sees that the same things can be grouped by a different
rule. Characters: Squirrel (left) and Deer (right).

## Stage 1 - story (children's room, after a snack)

1. Apples and pears lie mixed on the rug. Squirrel: apples to one container,
   pears to the other. A basket and a box appear. The child DRAGS things into them
   in any order and may take them out again; nothing is checked until everything
   is inside. Then each container must hold one kind: the odd ones shake and fly
   back out with Deer naming the rule again. Everyday bridge: spoons, socks, blocks.
2. Squirrel: "can we sort them another way?" The same fruit at two sizes, the rule
   is now big / small. Summary: the same things, different rules - say the rule
   first, then sort.

## Stage 2 - practice (Deer asks, Squirrel cheers)

Four rounds, six things each, basket + box: cups vs the rest (kind); things you
can eat vs the rest (purpose); red things vs the rest (colour: apple, ball);
small vs big (size). Same free dragging, same check.

## Stage 3 - apply (kitchen)

Squirrel and Deer tidy up after a snack on screen: Squirrel collects the food
into the basket, the child collects the dishes to the shelf; they end by asking
the child to ask the parents to tidy like this at home and then change the rule.
No card, no text.

## Notes

Colour rounds use only objects whose colour is unambiguous in the icons (red
apple, red-and-white ball). Containers are engine zones drawn over the basket
and box stickers; items inside are laid out in rows by their real widths, a
little smaller than loose, so the big ones fit too.
