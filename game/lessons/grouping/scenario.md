# Grouping - the mystery of the red gifts

Goal: group things by a named rule, and see that the same things can be grouped by different rules - and that the
right rule explains something (a detective's tool). Age 3-4. Characters: Squirrel (left), Deer (right), Fox (the shy
one, at his home in the oak). One walk across the panorama `jesienny_sad` (orchard - meadow - forest edge), autumn
leaves in the air; the story ends inside Fox's oak (`lisia_nora`).

**Misconceptions:** any rule will do - the first one tried (food / the rest) puts the gifts in both groups and tells us
nothing; "tidying = everything into one box" (Fox, at home) - then nothing can be found.

**The thread:** someone leaves red gifts under the apple tree; Deer: when we find who, we invite them home for an apple
pie. The story finds Fox; the home stage is his first visit and the pie (seen).

## Stage 1 - story - the mystery

A. **The orchard.** Gifts again under the apple tree, each with a golden bow (named one by one). We must find out who
   it is: be detectives, check what the gifts have in common - maybe it helps us guess who left them. A gust blows other
   things in among them (the gifts keep their bows). **Insert** on a plain colour (the method): apples and cones lie
   mixed, then the apples fly together and the cones together - Deer: grouping = things that belong together go
   together. Back in the orchard, rule one: **child** sorts food into the bowl, the rest into the box - the gifts end up
   in BOTH groups: not this rule. Rule two: **child** sorts red into the basket, the rest into the box - every gift is in
   the basket: they are all red! A clue: someone loves red. A trail of red leaves leads away.
B. **The meadow.** A cold wind: **child** points to what is useful against it (a hat and a scarf among a ball and a
   spoon) and drags them onto Squirrel; Deer has her fur (quadrupeds are never dressed). Along the trail - and then the
   wind heaps leaves over it: the trail is gone. *Breather* (`zdrapka` trop_pod_liscmi): **child** rakes the leaves off
   with a finger - paw prints! (circled) **Child** points where they lead: to the big oak in the forest. The forest is
   big: which way? The wind blows a map into a branch and tears it: **child** puts the torn map together (`podarty`
   mapa_lasu); three paths, each strewn with leaves of one colour - the giver loves red, so **child** walks our way along
   the path of red leaves (`wedrowka`, the other paths shake the pawn: other leaves). It ends at the big oak with a door.
C. **The forest edge.** "Look, what a beautiful red oak!" - "Somebody lives here..." - "Oh! And it has a red door. This
   must be the place!" Knock-knock: Fox comes out and walks up - it was him. How it happened, as a **comic** told by Fox
   (`lisek_prezenty`, panel by panel): every morning he gathered red things and tied golden bows; he laid them under the
   apple tree; he peeked from behind a tree. Why did he hide? He wanted to be friends but was shy. Thanks, and the
   invitation for the pie - but he cannot find his red hat in the mess. "Come inside."
D. **Inside Fox's home in the oak** (`lisia_nora`, the dedicated last place). "How cosy! Red curtains, a red armchair, a
   red rug..." - his hats and scarves lie all mixed up, turned. **Child** sorts them, hats into the basket, scarves into
   the box; **child** points to the red hat; Fox puts it on. Off to the pie.

## Stage 2 - practice - a detective's training (plain colours, five stars)
1. What fits this group? Three red things in Deer's bubble - **child** points to the one of two that joins them ("Tak,
   czerwona piłka...").
2. The secret rule (`regula`, cecha `rodzaj`): a basket and a chest with no label; **child** puts mushrooms and cones in;
   a wrong one is spat back; after three right ones Deer says the rule.
3. The odd one out: three acorns and a chestnut - named ("Tak, kasztan...").
4. **Your own rule** (`regula` with `cechy` kolor / rodzaj, on another colour): leaves and flags, red and yellow -
   **child** groups them as they like; the first drops decide and Squirrel says the rule the child chose.
5. **The same things another way**: the same leaves and flags, now by the OTHER rule; Deer: the same things, quite
   different groups. Five stars: a real detective.
Every right answer is named and said with the answer in the shot; a dialogue as a two-shot, the tasks wide.

## Stage 3 - at home: the third act (`pokoj`)
1. **Arrival with the thread**: Fox's first visit (in his red hat); the pie is still baking.
2. **The misconception again**: the room is a mess - books, toys and clothes on the floor. Fox "tidies": everything
   into one box. Deer wants to play ball while the pie bakes - where is it? Fox rummages and cannot find it. Squirrel:
   let's group - every group gets its own place.
3. **Interactions, all different**: **child** sorts on the painted places: books onto the window sill, toys onto the rug,
   clothes into the box (`sort`, sill and rug from `miejsca`); "where do I look for the ball?" - **child** points to the
   rug (the toys); Fox finds it at once. The detective game, the home game shown in play: Squirrel slips a sock among
   the toys - **child** points to what does not fit; the pie is ready - **child** points to everything we need for it
   (a plate, a cup, a spoon among a block and a book).
4. **The payoff SEEN**: the pie on the rug, then the event illustration `szarlotka_z_liskiem` - the tidy room, Fox in his
   red hat, the friends eating the pie.
5. **The home task**: Squirrel to the parents - tidy together, every group in its place, then one hides something that
   does not fit and the other finds it.
