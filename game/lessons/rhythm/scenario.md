# Rhythm - the friends' band

Goal: a rhythm is something that repeats in order, again and again; the child hears it, sees it, does it
with the body, finds where it breaks and continues it (AB, AAB, ABB, ABC, big-small). Age 4-5.
Characters: Fox (host), Squirrel, Owl, Deer.

One thread over the three stages: **this afternoon the parents come, and the friends play them a concert.**
The story gets the band ready (playing together, the garland, the dance), the practice is the rehearsal,
the home stage is the concert itself - in Fox's kitchen, on pots and cups.

The idea comes in three ways, one per adventure, so a child who misses it by ear gets it by eye or by body:
sound (drum and bell), picture (a garland of flags), movement (a dance). The method is the same every time
and is the hint after a miss: **play the rhythm from the start and listen for what comes next** - the row
plays itself (`ctx.rytm`): each thing bumps and sounds on its beat, left to right, up to the gap.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. Fox's garden - noise is not music (the concept comes in)
1. Fox runs in: the parents are coming this afternoon - let's play them a concert! A drum and a bell lie on
   the grass. Squirrel grabs both: "I'll play! Loud and a lot!" and bangs at random, faster and faster
   (bum dzyń dzyń bum bum dzyń...). Owl covers her ears (emotion: a jolt), Fox winces.
2. Owl: that is not music, that is noise - nobody knows what comes next, so nobody can play along or
   dance to it. Squirrel: then how? Owl: music has a RHYTHM. Squirrel: what is a rhythm? Owl: something
   that repeats, in order, again and again. I'll show you - it's a fun game.
3. Owl plays bum, dzyń, bum, dzyń - every sound puts its picture into a row over their heads as it is
   heard (the drum and the bell on stickers). Then she stops: "what comes next? Listen from the start."
   The row replays. **Child points** to the bell or the drum; the chosen one sounds and joins the row.
   Wrong: a sour clash, Squirrel winces, the row plays from the start again.
4. Squirrel tries her own: bum, bum, dzyń (AAB), twice. **Child points** to what comes next.
5. The three play it together (bum bum dzyń, everyone nodding) - it sounds like music. Fox: now our
   concert needs decorations.

### B. Fox's garden - the garland (a rhythm you can see)
6. A string between two trees. Squirrel hangs flags: red, yellow, red, yellow, red, red, yellow...
   Owl: "a garland has a rhythm too - let's look at it from the start." The row reads itself along the
   string (each flag bumps); on the wrong one Owl stops: "hmm, something is wrong here."
   **Child points** to the flag that breaks the rhythm (two reds side by side). It flips off the string
   and Squirrel puts the right one in.
7. Three places left on the string, flags in a basket (and one blue that does not belong).
   **Child drags** the flags into the places. The garland is ready; Fox rolls it up "for the kitchen".

### C. The breather, no maths - musical statues (`oddech: posagi`, kept from oddechy_plan.md)
8. Fox: before we go - a game! While the music plays everyone dances; when it stops, everyone freezes
   like a statue. Who moves, sits down. Music (melodyjka), they dance, music stops, they freeze - but one
   still wiggles. **Child points** to who moved (twice, a different one each time). Laughter.

### D. The lake meadow - helping Deer (a rhythm you do with your body)
9. They go to invite Deer. She stands by the lake, sad: she would love to come, but she does not know
   how to dance, and everyone will be dancing. Fox: a dance has a rhythm too - we'll teach you.
10. Fox dances: clap, clap, jump - clap, clap, jump - clap, clap... and stops. Cards of the three moves
    (clap / jump / spin icons) lie on the grass. **Child points** to the next move; Deer does it.
    Wrong: Deer tries it, stumbles (shake), "let's do it from the start" - Fox dances it again.
11. A second dance: clap, spin, clap, spin, clap... **Child points** to the next move; Deer does it
    alone, then the whole dance by herself, and lights up: I can dance! I'm coming to your concert!
12. Fox: let's go home and get ready - the parents will soon be here.

## Stage 2 - practice (the rehearsal, a plain colour with a calm motif, the star bar)

Five different games, one star each:
1. **Echo** - Owl plays bum, dzyń, dzyń (ABB) twice and stops; the child points to what comes next.
2. **Beads** - red, yellow, blue beads on a string (ABC), three empty places; the child drags the beads.
3. **The broken row** - leaf, leaf, acorn, leaf, leaf, acorn, leaf, acorn, acorn...; point to the mistake.
4. **Big drum, small drum** - big (low bum), small (high bum), big, small... the child points to the next;
   the sizes are heard as well as seen.
5. **Squirrel's dance** - jump, spin, spin (ABB); the child points to the next move, Squirrel does it.

## Stage 3 - at home: the concert (Fox's kitchen, this afternoon) - the third act

13. Arrival with the thread: "we're home, the parents will be here any minute - let's get the kitchen ready!"
    The garland from the garden goes up over the kitchen and Squirrel reads it (red, yellow, red, yellow).
14. The home problem: the drum and the bell stayed in the garden (Owl, a jolt). Fox: the kitchen has
    instruments too - a pot is our drum, a spoon in a cup our bell (each sounds).
15. The misconception again: Squirrel "I'll play on everything at once, loud and a lot!" - her notes pile into a
    row over her head, out of order. Deer is lost (sweat): "I can't dance to that." Owl reminds her (a two-shot):
    music has a rhythm. Squirrel: "oops, I forgot about the rhythm!" Owl plays her song (pot, cup, pot, cup...)
    in a row over her own head; each row has a pencil line under it, the two rows stand apart.
    **Child points** to the song Deer can dance to (any note of Owl's row). Wrong: Deer explains that nothing
    repeats there, Owl's song plays again. Right: Deer nods along to it, "I know what comes next".
16. The table for the parents: the band takes its things in the paws (the floor is free); on the painted table
    (`miejsca`: stol) a teacup, a cookie, a teacup, a cookie and two dashed places; a teacup and two cookies on
    the floor. **Child drags** what comes next onto the table (a wrong one shakes, "look from the start"); the
    spare cookie goes; Squirrel reads the table aloud, each thing bumping.
17. The parents come in (heard: a clap, never shown speaking); the instruments go back on the floor. Fox plays
    pot, cup, pot, cup and hands over: **the child plays on** three times (a miss replays the row).
18. Deer dances for the parents to the music, the moves joining a row as she dances: clap, clap, jump, clap,
    clap... - and freezes: she forgot (stage fright, a close-up). The band holds its things again; three move
    cards on the floor. **Child drags** the next move into the empty place at the end of the row; Deer jumps,
    "now I remember", and dances the whole dance while the row plays and the band nods.
19. The payoff, seen: the event illustration `koncert_dla_rodzicow` - the kitchen band in front, the four
    parents at the set table clapping, the red-yellow garland. Fox: the parents are clapping, the concert
    worked because we played in rhythm; Deer: I danced in front of everyone - this morning I couldn't.
20. The home game shown in play, on a plain colour: Fox and Squirrel do "clap, clap, jump" together, then Fox to
    the parents: a concert at home with a pot, a spoon and a rhythm, or a dance - clap, clap, jump. The sticker
    (the drum, from Deer). Goodbye.

## Iterations

- v1 was the old lesson's shape: three rows of things to finish in three places (a garland, a garden
  bed, a picnic). Critique: the same task six times, nobody needs the rhythm for anything, no sound in a
  lesson about rhythm, the home game unrelated to the story.
- v2 made it a concert, but the concert was on a forest stage, so the home stage had nothing to close.
  Moved the concert home (the parents come, a kitchen band): one thread from the first line to the last.
- v3: the rhythm only heard is too hard for some children, only seen is the old lesson. Each adventure now
  has its own way in (hear, see, do), and the replay of the row is both the method and the hint.
- Checked against "watch it as the child": everyone who speaks is on screen (the parents are only
  applause); the row of sounds builds on screen as it is heard, so the child can look back; the garland
  is read from the start along the string, so the mistake is found by the method, not by guessing; the
  statues round names who moved after the freeze, and the mover keeps wiggling until pointed at.
- v-next (2026-10-29), the lesson pass: the story keeps its shape and gets the editing pass - the method of
  adventure A (what a rhythm is, AB and AAB) and the dance lesson of adventure D now play on plain-colour
  inserts and come back to the garden / the lake; the Owl-Squirrel dialogue is a two-shot instead of close-up
  ping-pong; the judge's instruction of the statues is said in the wide shot; no wide shot is cut away at once;
  a right answer is named in a medium shot with the row still on screen. Practice: the same five games, the
  praise shots show the finished rows; "Wskaż to." / "Wskaż kartę." became whole sentences. The home stage was a
  stub of the same mechanic (play on, twice): it is now a third act - arrival with the garland, the noise
  misconception again (Squirrel) answered by choosing the song with a rhythm, the parents' table set in a rhythm
  on the painted table, the concert played on, Deer's forgotten move dragged into the gap, the parents'
  applause SEEN as an event illustration, and the home game shown in play before the line for the parents.
