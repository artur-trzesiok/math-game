## 21. Counting to 5 (new)

**Idea:** one number for each thing; the last number is how many (cardinality); the number of things does not change
when they are moved, spread or turned (conservation); five can be shown as a die, a hand, a row.
**Misconception:** spread out = more - Bear spreads Hedgehog's five rolls along the counter: "now there are more!".

- **Story.** Bear opens a bakery; Squirrel and Hedgehog help.
  1. *Kitchen.* Five rolls on a tray: **child** counts them (`count`); Bear spreads them out - "more!"; **child** counts
     again: still five. 
  2. Orders: "four cookies, please" - **child** packs exactly four into a bag (drag; a fifth jumps back).
  3. *Orchard.* Picking for the cakes: which basket has five (dots of a die, a hand, a row - the same five).
  4. *Breather:* Hedgehog hops - count the hops (`dzwieki`, 3-5).
  5. The first customers: each pays as many coins as the price (`sklepik`).
- **Practice:** how many (counting to 5, point the digit); give three rolls; which tray has five (spread vs close);
  memory of five (dots - digit); point to the fourth bun in a row.
- **Home:** set the table for five (five plates, then five cups - one for each). "Count five steps with your parents."

## 22. Numbers 6 to 10 (rethought)

**Idea:** count past five to ten, a counted thing moves aside (never counted twice); 6-10 as five and some more (a full
hand and fingers on the other); the digits 6-10; ten is written with two digits.
**Misconception:** losing track - Mole counts logs lying in a heap and counts some twice ("twelve!").

- **Story.** Beaver's dam has a gap; it needs ten logs.
  1. *Brook.* Mole counts the heap - "twelve!"; Owl: move each one aside as you count. **Child** counts the logs into
     a row (`count` with `rzad`): eight - two are missing.
  2. Five and more: the logs in two rows, five on top and the rest below (ten frame) - 6 = five and one ... 
  3. *Forest.* **Child** brings logs until there are ten (drag, counted as they arrive).
  4. *Breather:* jump along the stepping stones 0-10 to the number Beaver says (`os`).
  5. The dam is closed; the digits 6-10 written on the logs; 10 = one and zero.
- **Practice:** how many (6-10) - point the digit; give seven; trace 7; which is more, 8 or 6 (heaps); the stones to 9.
- **Home:** ten fingers - two hands; "count ten toys back into the box with your parents".

## 23. Adding to 5 (rethought)

**Idea:** adding = putting two groups together and counting all; the signs + and =; the order does not matter
(2 + 3 = 3 + 2).
**Misconception:** counting only the new ones - Bunny counts only the guests who just arrived.

- **Story.** Fox's picnic; guests keep coming.
  1. *Meadow.* Two guests are there, one more comes: Bunny "one!" (counts only the new one); Deer: put them together and
     count all. **Child** drags the newcomer to the blanket and counts all (three); 2 + 1 = 3 written.
  2. More guests: 3 + 2 - the child joins the groups, counts five; the equation with a gap - which card goes in.
  3. *Orchard.* Apples into one basket from two trees: 2 + 2 (the scale shows the pans are even? no - one thing at a time).
  4. *Breather:* dominoes - a tile's two halves together (dots) - point to the tile with five.
  5. The order trick: 1 + 4 and 4 + 1 - the same five.
- **Practice:** join and count (to 5); the missing result card; which domino has four; 2 + 3 and 3 + 2 - the same?; the
  shop: pay 2 + 1 coins.
- **Home:** the kitchen: two spoons and two forks - how many on the table; "add with your parents: two apples and one".

## 24. Adding to 10 (rethought)

**Idea:** counting on - start from the bigger known number and count only the new ones; equations to 10.
**Misconception:** counting all from one every time (slow) - or counting only the new ones (Squirrel's "three!").

- **Story.** The hopscotch tournament.
  1. *Playground.* Squirrel has five pebbles (a pencil 5 on her bag), Owl brings three: Squirrel says "three!". Owl: the
     five are known - start from five: six, seven, eight (`count` with `od`). 5 + 3 = 8.
  2. Hopscotch along the stones: from 6, hop 3 more - where do you land? (`os`)
  3. *Brook.* Bear's bucket of fish - the bucket holds 7, he catches 2 more.
  4. *Breather:* the bell - Owl rings 6 times, then 2 more - how many in all (sound).
  5. The tournament ends: points added on the board.
- **Practice:** count on from 6; hop from 4 with 4 more; the missing card in 7 + 2 = _; shop: 8 coins; which sum is 10.
- **Home:** the hallway: 6 shoes and 2 more; "count on with your parents: from five, three more steps".

## New things this stage needs

Mechanics (to agree first):
- `ramka` - a ten frame: two rows of five places; things dropped in fill it place by place; the child sees "five and
  some more" at a glance. Used in 22 (6-10 as five and more), 24 (counting on to 10); later any number lesson.
- `domino` - domino tiles with two halves of dots, as task targets and as cards for a match ("which tile has five?").
  Could be done with the existing drawn dots (kropki) and no new mechanic - just a sticker built in code.

Without new mechanics: counting (`count`, `rzad`, `od`), giving exactly n (drag), the shop (`sklepik`), the stepping
stones (`os`), sounds (`dzwieki`), digits and tracing (`digit`, `slad`), equations with gaps (`equation`), memory.

Stickers: rolls (bulka), cookies are there (ciastko), a paper bag, a tray, logs (bal), a dam, a bucket, fish, dominoes
(drawn in code), plates and cups exist.

# New lessons, 2026-10-30

Built like every lesson now: three stages, about five interactions each in different forms, a misconception voiced by
a character, a non-maths breather, the home stage as a third act in a painted room with `miejsca`, the payoff seen.
Counting up to five is allowed (counting_5 comes first in the stage), no digits past 5, no sums.

## 22. Light and heavy (light_heavy)

**Idea:** heavy and light are felt (hands, a raft that dips) and seen on a balance: the heavier pan goes DOWN; size
does not tell weight; three things can be put in order from the lightest.
**Misconception:** bigger = heavier - Bunny: "the big pillow is the heaviest, the little stone weighs nothing".

- **Story.** Beaver built a raft to take the picnic across the brook; it tips if the load is wrong.
  1. *Brook.* Bear steps on the raft - it dips; Bunny steps on - it hardly moves: **child** points to who is heavier.
  2. *Workshop.* Beaver's balance (`waga`, first use): **child** puts the stone and the pillow on the pans; Bunny bets on
     the pillow (misconception) - the stone's pan goes down. Insert (plain colour): down = heavier.
  3. Pairs on the balance (pumpkin / feather, apple / walnut): **child** says first which pan will go down, then puts.
  4. *Breather:* `ktoPowiedzial` - who said "the raft is ready"? (voices).
  5. Loading the raft: heavy things in the middle (sort into "middle" / "edges"), **child** orders three bags from the
     lightest (`slots`); the raft crosses - event illustration: the picnic on the far bank.
- **Practice:** which is heavier (point, pairs that look misleading); the balance: which pan goes down; order three by
  weight; balance two light things against one heavy (the beam levels); who can carry it - Bunny or Bear.
- **Home (third act).** The shopping from the picnic is brought home (the thread). Bunny wants the big bag of
  crisps at the bottom and the small bag of potatoes on top - "the big one is heavy!" (the misconception again).
  Interactions: which bag is heavier (two characters holding them, arms sag); unpack onto the kitchen shelves
  (`miejsca`: heavy on the low shelf, light on the high one - the shelves are empty since 2026-10-30); order three jars
  on the balance; a scratch-off (what is in the heaviest bag?). Payoff: nothing falls, Bear lifts the heavy pot onto
  the stove. Parents: "take two things in your hands - which is heavier?".

## 23. Will it fit? - thick and wide (thick_wide, title "Czy się zmieści?")

**Idea:** thick / thin for round things (logs, trunks, crayons) and wide / narrow for flat things and openings (gates,
paths, ribbons); whether it fits is decided by thickness or width, not length; order three by thickness.
**Misconception:** longer = wider / thicker - Fox: "the long pole won't go through, the short barrel will".

- **Story.** Mole moves into a new burrow; Fox and Deer help carry.
  1. *Garden gate.* The narrow gate (`szpara`, `szpara_furtka`, first use): Fox bets the short fat barrel goes through
     and the long thin pole does not - **child** pushes them through: the pole passes, the barrel sticks. Insert: wide
     and narrow.
  2. The wide gateway (`szpara_brama`): **child** picks which gate the barrel goes through.
  3. *Birch wood.* Between two birches (`szpara_drzewa`): which of three logs fits (thick / thin); **child** points to
     the thickest trunk in the wood.
  4. *Breather:* `zdrapka` - mud off the doormat of the new burrow: what is drawn on it?
  5. *Mole's burrow.* Down the round tunnel only thin things go: **child** sorts thick / thin; the housewarming - event
     illustration: everything inside.
- **Practice:** which is thicker / thinner (trunks, crayons); which is wider / narrower (ribbons, paths); does it fit
  (`szpara`); order three crayons from the thinnest; wide or thick? - which word fits (two pictures).
- **Home (third act).** Mole's housewarming gifts go into the hallway (the thread): a wide picture, a thick book, a
  long thin umbrella. Deer: "the umbrella is so long, it won't go into the narrow stand" (the misconception again).
  Interactions: the umbrella into the narrow stand, the wide picture onto the wide hook (`miejsca`); order three books
  by thickness on the shelf; point to the widest scarf on the hook; which gift needs the door opened wide. Payoff: the
  hallway tidy, the guests come in. Parents: "find something thicker than your finger and something thinner".

## 24. The missing piece (complete_shape)

**Idea:** a gap has a shape; the piece that fits has the same shape (and turn), not the same colour; a whole picture
can be put together from pieces.
**Misconception:** colour decides - Squirrel: "the red piece fits, the hole is in the red roof" (wrong shape).

- **Story.** Owl painted a picture for Hedgehog's birthday; the wind tears it on the way.
  1. *Owl's oak.* The picture is ready (found picture); the wind blows it away - the pieces fall over the meadow.
  2. *Meadow.* The torn picture (`podarty`, first use, 4 pieces): **child** puts it back together; one piece is still
     missing - a hole.
  3. Three candidate pieces (stickers painted for the hole): Squirrel picks the red one by colour - it does not fit;
     **child** points to the one of the right SHAPE. Insert: look at the edges, not the colour.
  4. *Breather:* `malowanie` - colour the frame for the picture.
  5. A gap in the bridge plank (`piece` on a tangram silhouette: which piece fills the gap), then the birthday - event
     illustration: Hedgehog gets the mended picture.
- **Practice:** which piece fits the gap (shape, not colour); turn the piece to fit (`tangram`, tap to turn); a torn
  picture of 6 pieces; what is missing from the picture (a cup without its handle - point to the handle); a tangram
  silhouette from three pieces.
- **Home (third act).** Hedgehog's birthday tea in the room (the thread). A plate falls and breaks: Squirrel wants
  the blue piece "because the plate is blue" (the misconception again). Interactions: mend the plate (which piece
  fits); the puzzle game on the floor (`tangram`, a house); what is missing from the birthday card (point); a
  scratch-off (the gift under the paper). Payoff: Hedgehog hangs the picture on the wall (`miejsca`), everyone claps.
  Parents: "cut a picture into four pieces and put it together with your child".
