# Tall and short - the treehouse

Goal: height - tall / short, taller / shorter, the tallest / the shortest; the method: everyone on ONE ground, a line
over each head. Age 3-4. Characters: Bear (new, the tallest), Squirrel, Fox; Hedgehog (new, the shortest). Stage 2,
lesson 5.

**Misconception:** whoever stands higher up is taller (Squirrel on a stump: "now I'm the tallest!"; at home Hedgehog
on a pile of books).

One thread: **Bear wants to see the whole forest from above** - the story gets him into the treehouse and the wish
comes true (the view from the window); Hedgehog, the shortest, never saw it; at home Bear keeps the promise.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. The forest - higher up is not taller (the concept comes in)
1. Bear sees the treehouse (medium): I'd love to see the whole forest from above. Fox (wide, they come in): the
   lowest branch is high - which of us is the tallest and can reach it?
2. Squirrel climbs a stump (the stump in the shot): now I'm the tallest! She jumps - and does not reach (close-up,
   sad). Fox: the stump only lifted you up; to check who is tall, we stand on the same ground.
3. **Insert on a plain colour (the method):** the three stand on ONE pencil line; Fox draws a line over each head.
   **Child** points to the tallest (Bear: I'll reach the branch!) and to the shortest (Squirrel: but I climb fastest).

### B. The ladder
4. Back in the forest: the treehouse ladder is broken (Bear, sad); we need a tall stick. **Child** stands both
   sticks on the pencil line (one ground), then points to the taller one. The tall stick
   flies to the ladder - mended; Bear: up we go!

### C. Inside the treehouse (new scenery)
5. Bear runs to the round window: **event illustration** - the whole forest from above, the orchard right below. The
   wish kept.
6. Squirrel finds an old chest; locked; the key on the highest peg - Squirrel jumps, cannot reach. **Child** points
   to the tallest - Bear takes it down, the chest opens: a spyglass (a discovery).
7. Through the spyglass the orchard below - **child** finds the hidden mice (`szukaj`, no number said). Hedgehog
   waves from the orchard.

### D. The orchard - helping Hedgehog
8. Hedgehog would like an apple, but he is very short. **Child** points to the shortest tree - he reaches it (heart).
9. *Breather, no maths - "who said that?":* Hedgehog: let's play hide and seek! Hedgehog, Squirrel and Fox hide
   behind the bushes, Bear seeks; a voice calls "peekaboo!" - **child** points to the bush it came from.
10. Hedgehog, sad: I'm too short for the ladder, I never saw the forest from above. Bear: come home with us - we'll
    think of something.

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert, drifting clouds, five stars)
Each round: a medium shot on the things before the question, the instruction wide, a close-up on the praise.
1. Two candles stood on one shelf: which is taller? 2. A sunflower up on a stump: stand both on the line first, then
which is taller? 3. The shortest of three sticks. 4. Whose shadow - the tall tree's or the short one's? 5. The friends:
who is the shortest of us?

## Stage 3 - at home - the third act (the hallway, painted places)
11. Arrival with the thread (two-shot Bear + Hedgehog): Hedgehog came along; Bear brought the spyglass home. **Child**
    hangs the spyglass on the painted hook rail - high, where it is safe.
12. Fox: the height chart on the wall - everyone stands by it, a pencil line over each head (Bear, Squirrel, Fox are
    marked, each one walking to the chart).
13. The home problem, the misconception again: Hedgehog climbs a pile of books - "now I'll be taller than Squirrel!"
    Squirrel objects (two-shot): the books only lift you. **Child** puts the books away onto the painted shoe rack;
    Hedgehog comes down to the floor with each one; his line is drawn.
14. **Child** points to the highest line on the chart (the lines themselves are the options) - Bear's; then **child**:
    who is taller, Fox or Squirrel?
15. The payoff: Hedgehog, the shortest, sighs about the forest. Bear (the tallest, he reaches the hooks) takes the
    spyglass down and lifts Hedgehog onto his shoulders - **event illustration**: Hedgehog at the round window with the
    spyglass, seeing the whole forest; "now I'm the highest of all - but not the tallest!" Next year his line will be
    higher. Fox: measure yourself with your parents by the door and mark a line - who is the tallest at home?

## Iterations
- v1/v2: the treehouse adventure chain (stump, ladder, chest and key, spyglass, orchard), the height chart at home.
- v3 (2026-10-29, the improvement plan): one thread (Bear's wish to see the forest from above) kept in the story by
  an event illustration (the forest from the treehouse window) and at home by a second one (Hedgehog on Bear's
  shoulders with the spyglass - higher, not taller); the method shown on a plain-colour insert (one line, a line over
  every head); the sunflower catch became "who said that?" (hide and seek in the orchard); the home stage became a
  third act on the painted hallway (the spyglass on the hook rail, the books onto the shoe rack, the highest line on
  the chart, Fox or Squirrel, the payoff seen); the sticks stand on the line through dashed places (counted by the
  preview); practice and home edited shot by shot; every "Wskaż." made one sentence; "six mice" lost its number (no
  counting past three before it is taught); "dotknij" became "wskaż".
