# Counting to 3 - Hedgehog's birthday

Goal: count to three by touch, each thing once; the last number tells how many; the digits 1, 2, 3. Age 3-4.
Characters: Hedgehog, Bear, Squirrel. Stage 3, lesson 10.

**Misconception:** counting a thing twice - Bear touches the middle candle twice and says "four!". At home it comes
back: Hedgehog counts his guests and counts Bear twice - four plates for three friends.

**One thread:** Hedgehog turns three; Bear promises him a raspberry cake. The story fetches what the cake needs (how
many raspberries - Grandma's recipe says), the home stage is the party: the table laid for exactly three, the cake as in
the recipe, three candles, and Hedgehog blowing them out (seen).

## Stage 1 - story
A. *Meadow.* Hedgehog is three today. Bear promises a raspberry cake and brings candles; he counts "one, two, three,
four" (the middle one bumps twice). Squirrel: he counted the middle one twice - each thing once, the last number says
how many. **Child** counts the candles by touch (`count`): three, as old as Hedgehog. The method on a plain-colour
**insert**: the digits 1, 2, 3 drawn one after another, each named; **child** traces the 3 (`slad`). Bear: let's go to
the raspberry thicket for the cake.
B. *The raspberry thicket* (a scenery of its own, the end of the story). They arrive and look around: bushes full of
raspberries. But how many go on the cake? Bear has Grandma's recipe - a found picture (`znalezisko` przepis_tort);
**child** counts its raspberries by touch, each once - three. Squirrel knows the sweetest ones hide under the leaves:
**child** rubs the leaves off a branch (`zdrapka` maliny_pod_liscmi) and **child** counts the ripe raspberries under
them - three, just as in the recipe; Bear picks them into his basket. *Breather* (`kropki` bebenek_prezent, from the
smallest dot, no numbers): Squirrel has a surprise present for Hedgehog - **child** joins the dots and a little drum
appears. Bear: we have the raspberries, home to bake the cake.

## Stage 2 - practice
How many apples (a digit card); give Squirrel two acorns into her bowl (`rozdaj`, a third one is refused); trace 1 and
2; how many drum beats (a digit card); which heap has three pears. Each right answer is named ("Tak, dwa jabłka!");
five stars.

## Stage 3 - at home: the third act (`dom`, the kitchen)
1. **Arrival with the thread**: home, the raspberry cake is baking - let's lay the table for the party.
2. **The misconception again**: Hedgehog counts the guests and points at Bear twice - "four!", four plates come out.
   Squirrel: you counted Bear twice; Bear: each one once - let's check, everyone gets one plate.
3. **Interactions, all different**: on a plain-colour **insert** (the method: one each) **child** gives each friend one
   plate (`rozdaj`, one each) - one plate is left over, there are three of us; back in the kitchen **child** puts the
   three plates on the painted table (`miejsca` stol, three places); the cake comes - **child** counts its raspberries,
   as many as in Grandma's recipe (`count`); how many candles? **child** points to the digit of Hedgehog's age (3), and
   three candles stand on the cake.
4. **The payoff SEEN**: the event illustration `zdmuchiwanie_swieczek` - Hedgehog blows out his three candles, Bear
   claps, Squirrel throws confetti, three plates, the drum by his plate.
5. **The home task shown in play**: three birthday hops, all together, counted out loud; Bear: count to three with your
   parents - three stairs, three apples, three hops.
