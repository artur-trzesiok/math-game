# In order - Beaver's evening concert

Goal: put things in order by size, from the biggest to the smallest and back, and see why order matters. Age 3-4.
Characters: Beaver, Bunny, Owl (and her owlets). Stage 2, lesson 6.

## Why rethought (twice)
The first lesson was three unconnected rows; the second had one good consequence (a tower stands only in order) but the
practice and the home stage were the same drag-into-places game, "without flair". Now ONE thread runs through all three
stages - tonight Beaver's forest concert - and order matters in three different ways: a tower stands, a path crosses,
a tune sounds right. Two of them are heard, not seen.

**Misconception:** "a row is a row" - Bunny piles blocks anyhow (the tower falls) and swaps two owlets for a joke (the
choir sings out of tune).

## Stage 1 - story
A. **The brook.** Beaver: a concert tonight, first a tower for a lantern so the guests find the way. Bunny piles a big
block on a small one - it tips and falls. In order: the biggest at the bottom. **Child** builds the tower (`wieza`);
the lantern goes up and lights (`rozblysk`).
B. **The map** (`znalezisko`): Beaver's old map of the way to the glade flies up big; **child** marks where the dotted
path crosses the brook - that is where the way across goes. Owl's owlets wait on the far bank and cannot fly yet.
**Child** lays stepping stones into the water from the lowest (by the owlets) to the highest; the owlets hop across
one after another and grow as they come near.
C. **The concert glade** (new scenery `polana_koncert`). The choir rehearsal: **child** lines the owlets up from the
smallest; they sing a short wordless owl song (ElevenLabs `chor_sowiatek`). Bunny swaps two - the song goes sour
(`chor_falsz`); **child** finds an owlet out of its place; back in order, it sounds right again. Beaver: home now, to
get his instrument ready.

## Stage 2 - practice
Cups from the smallest; carrots from the biggest; the apple that fits the gap; which row of leaves is in order; a tower of three.

## Stage 3 - at home (the room, evening)
Beaver's water-glass xylophone (`szklanka_1..4`, tuned notes `szklo_1..4` = C E G C). **Child** taps every glass and
hears each is different (more water - lower); puts them in order from the fullest; they play a rising scale "like
steps"; **child** plays it, glass after glass in order. "With your parents, pour water into a few glasses, put them in
order and listen to them play."
