# Stage 3 - the plan (noticing order, counting to 3, more and fewer, what goes with what)

The leaf stage, planned like stages 1 and 2. Here the first numbers come in (counting to 3), so the stage moves from
seeing to counting; each lesson has its own places, cast and kind of story.

| | idea | thread | places | cast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | noticing order (first, then, last) | Owl's picture book fell apart | forest, garden, room | Owl, Bunny, Fox |
| 10 | counting to 3 | Hedgehog's birthday | meadow, forest, kitchen | Hedgehog, Bear, Squirrel |
| 11 | more and fewer | the night of the fireflies (kept) | glade at night, room at night | Fox, Squirrel, Owl, Deer |
| 12 | what goes with what | the storm mixed everybody's things | brook, Beaver's workshop, hallway | Beaver, Mole, Deer |

## 9. Noticing order (new)

**Idea:** things happen in order - first, then, last; a story can be told from its pictures; what is missing.
**Misconception:** "any order is fine" - Bunny puts the pages back anyhow and the story makes no sense (the flower
grows before the seed is planted).

- **Story.** Owl reads a picture book to Bunny; Bunny, in a hurry, drops it - the pages fly.
  1. *Forest.* Bunny gathers them anyhow; Owl reads them in that order and it is nonsense (the chick before the egg -
     everybody laughs). Owl: a story goes in order - first, then, last. **Child** puts three pages in order (`slots`).
  2. *Garden.* The story's next page happens for real: Fox plants a seed - the child watches it grow (a sprout, a
     bud, a flower appear one after another); then the pictures of it are mixed - **child** puts them in order.
  3. *Helping.* Fox's snowman picture is missing a page: which one fits between (the middle step).
  4. *Breather:* Bunny's hop race - who is first, who is last (pointing at characters).
  5. The book is whole again; Owl reads it from the start (each page bumps as it is read: `rytm` with a line).
- **Practice:** order three pictures (a cake: ingredients, bowl, cake); what came first (two pictures); what is
  missing in a row of four; first / last in a queue of animals; memory of story steps.
- **Home:** getting dressed in order (pants, trousers, shoes); "tell your parents a story in three pictures".

## 10. Counting to 3 (new)

**Idea:** one, two, three - counting things by touch, each once; the last number says how many; the digits 1, 2, 3.
**Misconception:** counting the same thing twice (Bear counts a candle twice and says "four!").

- **Story.** Hedgehog's birthday - he is three.
  1. *Meadow.* Hedgehog: I'm three! Bear brings candles for the cake and counts - one, two, three, four... he
     touched one twice (it grows each time - the old lessons' trick). Squirrel: touch each one once. **Child**
     counts three candles by touch (`count`).
  2. The digits: 1, 2, 3 drawn with the pencil, then **child** traces the 3 (`slad`).
  3. *Forest, helping.* Bear gathers three berries for the cake - **child** gives him exactly three (drag; a fourth
     jumps back).
  4. *Breather:* Hedgehog claps for each friend who comes - count the claps (`dzwieki`).
  5. *Kitchen.* The cake: three candles, everybody sings.
- **Practice:** how many (1-3) - point to the digit card; give two acorns to the squirrel; trace 1 and 2; listen -
  how many drum beats (`dzwieki`); a row of 3 - point to the third.
- **Home:** count three spoons on the table; "count to three with your parents: stairs, apples, jumps".

## 11. More and fewer - kept

The lantern night (fireflies, a game of stumps, practice games, a pillow fight) is recent and built on the
guidelines; about 3 minutes of story speech - kept, with one look at its length when the stage is played through.

## 12. What goes with what (new)

**Idea:** logical pairs - things that belong together (key-lock, sock-shoe, cup-saucer, brush-paint, bee-flower,
hammer-nail); why they go together.
**Misconception:** pairing by look (same colour, same size) instead of by use - Mole, who sees badly, pairs the red
cup with the red apple.

- **Story.** A storm blew everybody's things into Beaver's workshop.
  1. *Brook.* Things everywhere after the storm; Mole tries to help by feel and colour - the red cup with a red
     apple (naive try). Deer: these two go together because we use them together - a cup with its saucer.
  2. *Workshop.* **Child** pairs things (the new mechanic below, or dragging one onto its pair).
  3. *Helping.* Deer's door won't open - which key fits the lock (three keys, the shapes of their bits).
  4. *Breather:* catching falling leaves after the storm.
  5. Everybody gets their things back.
- **Practice:** connect pairs; which one does not belong to the pair; memory of pairs; what is missing (the bee is
  there, its flower?); shadows of pairs.
- **Home:** socks to shoes in the hallway; "play 'what goes together' with your parents".

## New mechanic for acceptance

**`polacz` - joining pairs with a line drawn by a finger.** Two columns of things; the child draws from one thing to
its pair, the pencil follows the finger; a right pair keeps its line (and both bump), a wrong one wobbles and the line
fades. The classic way to show "these two go together", reusable for counting (a number to a group), for shadows and
for more-or-fewer (one to one).

Without it, lesson 12 drags one thing onto its pair (exists today).

New pictures: story-page sets (egg-chick-hen, seed-sprout-flower, snowman in three steps, cake in three steps,
getting dressed), birthday cake and candles, berries, keys and a lock, pair things (sock-shoe, cup-saucer, brush-
paint, bee-flower, hammer-nail), Beaver's workshop scenery.
