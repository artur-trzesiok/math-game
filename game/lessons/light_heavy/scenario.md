# Light and heavy

Goal: heavy and light are felt (a raft that dips) and seen on a balance - the heavier pan goes DOWN; size does not tell
weight; three things can be put in order from the lightest. Age 3-4. Characters: Beaver (left, the builder), Bunny
(left, the hasty one), Bear (right, the heavy one). Stage 6, after counting_5: the child knows counting and digits to
5 and the comparisons of size; weight is new.

**Misconception:** bigger = heavier - Bunny: "the big pillow is the heaviest, the little stone weighs nothing".

One thread: **Beaver's raft must carry the picnic across the brook** - it tips if loaded wrong, so the friends find out
what is heavy (the workshop's balance) and load it right; the picnic on the far bank ends with Bear's invitation to
pumpkin soup at his home, where the shopping is unpacked (Bunny's mistake again) and the soup is cooked.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. The brook - the raft dips (the concept comes in)
1. Beaver and Bunny at the brook, the new raft floats by the bank (two-shot, then a medium shot on Beaver and the raft):
   "I built a raft! We'll take the picnic across the brook." Careful: it tips when it is loaded wrong.
2. Bear: "Me first!" - he hops onto the raft (wide): it sinks deep with a splash. Bunny (close-up, "!"): "Oh! It went
   down deep!" Bear hops back; Bunny hops on - the raft hardly moves (medium on Bunny and the raft): "Under me it
   hardly moved!"
3. **Child** points to who is heavier - Bear or Bunny (wide, both on the bank). Beaver names it: Bear is heavier; under
   the heavier one the raft sinks deeper.
4. Bear (two-shot with Beaver): and our things - the watermelon, the pot, the blanket? Which are heavy? Beaver: we'll
   check on my balance - to the workshop!

### B. The workshop - Beaver's balance (first use of `waga`)
5. The balance stands on the floor (medium on Beaver and the balance): two pans; we put things on and watch. A little
   stone and a big pillow float over the heads.
6. Bunny (medium on Bunny and the pillow): "The pillow is huge, so it's the heaviest! The stone is tiny, it weighs
   nothing!" Bear: let's check. **Child** puts the stone on one pan and the pillow on the other (drag) - the stone's pan
   goes DOWN. Bunny (close-up, "!"): "What? The stone's pan went down!"
7. **Insert (plain colour):** the balance alone with the stone and the pillow, a pencil arrow at the low pan. Beaver:
   the pan that goes down is heavier, the one that goes up is lighter. The stone is small but heavy, the pillow big but
   light. Bunny (close-up): "Aha! Big doesn't mean heavy."
8. Back in the workshop, guess first: a pumpkin and a feather over the pans. **Child** points to what is heavier, then
   puts both on the pans and sees the pumpkin's pan go down. Again with an apple and a nut (apple down).
9. Bear (two-shot): now we know how to check what is heavy - back to the brook!

### C. The brook again - loading the raft, the crossing
10. *Breather, no maths* (`ktoPowiedzial`): Bunny wants to play hide-and-seek before sailing; Beaver seeks, Bear and
    Bunny hide behind bushes, a voice calls "a kuku!" and the **child** points to the bush it came from.
11. Loading (wide, the raft on the water): a balloon (the biggest), an apple and a watermelon on the bank. **Child**
    puts them in order from the lightest (places over the heads, an arrow at the first). Beaver names it: balloon,
    apple, watermelon - though the balloon is the biggest. Bunny eats the apple (it is not cargo).
12. The pot and the blanket join the balloon and the watermelon. Beaver: heavy things in the middle, light ones on the
    edges - then it won't tip. **Child** loads the raft (two places in the middle, one on each edge). Beaver names it.
    Bear: "And me?" - "You sit right in the middle!"
13. **Event illustration** `tratwa_przeprawa`: the raft crosses level, Bear and the heavy things in the middle, the
    balloon and the blanket at the edges.

### D. The picnic glade (the story's own place)
14. The far bank (`polana_piknik`), the picnic blanket: Bear - we're across, nothing fell in. Beaver (close-up): thank
    you, without you the raft would have tipped. Bear (two-shot with Bunny): after the picnic we'll do the shopping
    and I'll cook pumpkin soup at my home - you're invited! (the thread to the home stage)

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert on a soft green, drifting circles, five stars) - "the weighing school"
1. **Which is heavier** - misleading pairs: a big balloon or a small stone; a big pillow or a small hammer (point).
2. **The balance:** a big stone over one pan, a leaf over the other - **child** points to the PAN that will go down,
   then Beaver puts them on and the beam tips.
3. **In order:** a leaf, an apple and a pumpkin (the leaf drawn biggest) - from the lightest to the heaviest (places,
   an arrow at the first).
4. **Level:** a honey jar on one pan; **child** puts apples on the other until the beam is level (two apples; a third
   tips it the other way - Bear says "too many", one comes off). "Level" = they weigh the same.
5. **Who can carry it:** the heavy log - Bear or Bunny (point to the character); the balloon lifts only someone light -
   who is lighter? (Bunny floats up a little).

## Stage 3 - at home - the third act (Bear's kitchen, `dom`, the painted wall shelves)
15. Arrival with the thread (two-shot Bear / Beaver): home with the shopping - unpack it, then the soup.
16. The home problem with the same misconception: a big puffy crisps bag and a small potato bag. Bunny: "The big bag
    is heavy - the bottom shelf! The little one goes on top!" - and puts the crisps on the low shelf. Beaver (close-up,
    "?"): does big mean heavy? Remember the stone and the pillow!
17. Bear: let's check in our paws - Bear takes the potatoes (sweats), Bunny the crisps (hops). **Child** points to the
    heavier bag. Bear names it: the potatoes - a small bag, but heavy.
18. Two jars: a big popcorn jar and a small honey jar. Bunny: "This time I won't guess - the balance!" **Child** puts
    both on the pans; the honey jar goes down. Bunny names it.
19. Beaver: heavy things on the low shelf, light ones on the high shelf, so nothing falls. **Child** unpacks onto the
    painted shelves (`miejsca`: two places on each). Bear names it.
20. Bear's heaviest bag lies under brown paper on the table: **child** rubs it off (scratch-off `zakupy_misia`) - a huge
    pumpkin! **Child** points to what makes the bag so heavy.
21. The payoff, **event illustration** `zupa_dyniowa`: Bear lifts the heavy soup pot onto the stove, the shopping on
    the shelves (heavy low, light high), Bunny at the table.
22. The home game shown: Bunny holds a nut, Bear an apple - the apple is heavier. Beaver (close-up) to the parents:
    take two things in your hands - which is heavier?

## Iterations
- v1 (2026-10-30), from the plan in etap6_plan.md ("New lessons", 22). Changes from the plan: the three things put in
  order on the raft are a balloon, an apple and a watermelon instead of three bags - identical bags give a child
  nothing to judge weight by, and this way the biggest thing is the lightest; the breather's voice is the shared pool's
  "a kuku!" (the `ktoPowiedzial` pool has no "the raft is ready" line); home weighs two jars (big light popcorn, small
  heavy honey) instead of ordering three - the misconception again, decided by the balance; the story ends on the
  picnic glade (`polana_piknik`) after the crossing illustration, so it ends somewhere new.
