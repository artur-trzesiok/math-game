# Morning and night - Mole's long day

Goal: the parts of the day in order - morning, midday, evening, night - and what we do in each; before / after.
Age 3-4. Characters: Mole (new), Deer, Squirrel. Stage 2, lesson 8.

**Misconception:** Mole lives underground and sees badly - he thinks it is always night. At home it comes back as "it
is evening, so everything at once": he puts his pyjamas on and jumps into the bath in them.

**One thread:** Deer and Squirrel promise Mole a whole day, from the morning to the night. The day ends at the old oak
under the stars; Mole stays the night at his friends' and - for the first time - wakes up in the morning with them.

## Stage 1 - story
A. *Burrow* (`nora`, dim). Mole wakes: is it morning? Underground it's always dark - he thinks it is always night.
Squirrel and Deer come down the tunnel: "Always dark? Your way out is full of earth - it fell in during the night, so no
light gets in." **Breather** (`zdrapka` wyjscie_z_nory): **child** rubs the earth off the round way out; behind it the
sun is rising over the meadow and a rooster is crowing on the fence. **Child** points to who crows so loud (the
rooster): "the rooster crows in the morning - get up!". Mole: so it is not always night? Deer: come up with us, we'll
show you the whole day - morning, midday, evening and night.
B. *Meadow.* The dark lifts while they climb out; the **event illustration** `wschod_slonca`: Mole half out of his
molehill at sunrise, a paw over his eyes - "Oh, how bright! The sun is rising." Deer: in the morning we get up, wash
and eat breakfast. **Child** points to what we do in the morning (breakfast, among the bed and pyjamas). The method on a
plain-colour **insert**: the four cards of the day come in one by one while Deer says how the day goes (the sun rises,
high up, sets, the moon). Back on the meadow Squirrel has drawn Mole's day for him - a gust scatters the pictures;
**child** puts the **story strip** `dzien_kreta` in order (an arrow at the first place); Squirrel names it: in the
morning Mole gets up, at midday he plays ball, in the evening he walks home, at night he sleeps.
C. Evening falls (`noc`). *Helping:* little Hedgehog is lost in the bushes - **child** shines a light on a bush
(`swiatlo`); an empty one stays lit. Squirrel knows him: he lives under the old oak across the wood; Mole: it is getting
darker, after the evening comes the night - we must hurry. Deer: many paths, a lantern, the right path. **Child** leads
the little hedgehog through the forest paths at dusk to his door (`labirynt` las_o_zmierzchu).
D. *The old oak at night* (`dab_noc`, the dedicated last place: moon, stars, a little lit door in the roots, fireflies).
The little hedgehog walks in through his door - home before the night. Mole has never seen the night sky: **child** joins
the stars from the smallest (`kropki` niedzwiedz_gwiazd, `wielkosc`) and the Great Bear appears. Mole: after the morning
the midday, after the midday the evening, after the evening the night - and after the night the morning again. Squirrel:
tonight you sleep at ours, and in the morning we wake up together.

## Stage 2 - practice
What comes after evening; what we do at night; three cards in order; memory of day cards; what comes after night. Each
right answer named ("Yes, the bed - at night we sleep."); five stars.

## Stage 3 - at home: the third act (`pokoj_noc`, the evening at Squirrel's)
1. **Arrival with the thread**: it is evening, Mole sleeps over and will wake up with them in the morning.
2. **The misconception again**: "Evening? Pyjamas on at once - and into the bath!" He jumps into the tub in his pyjamas:
   splash, wet (`mokro`). The method on an **insert**: the bath and the pyjamas alone - first the bath, then the
   pyjamas; in the evening we do things in order too.
3. **Interactions, all different**: **child** points to what comes first in the evening (the bath); **child** puts the
   whole evening in order on the painted rug (`miejsca` dywan: bath, pyjamas, story, bed); Squirrel reads a bedtime story
   and **child** puts the book away on the painted bedside table by the lamp (`miejsca` szafka_nocna); the light goes
   down, goodnight; **child** points to what comes after the night (the morning card).
4. **The payoff SEEN**: the event illustration `pobudka_rano` - the next morning, the sun in the window, Mole sits up in
   bed stretching, wide awake, Squirrel waves from her cushion nest, Deer opens the curtain. Mole woke up in the morning.
5. **The home task shown in play**: the four cards of the day on the rug; **child** points to them in order and for each
   Mole tells what he did then (the earth and the sun, the pictures on the meadow, Hedgehog home, the Great Bear and the
   sleepover). Deer: "Now tell your parents what you did this morning, at midday and in the evening."

## Iterations
- **v-next, 2026-10-29** (lesson pass): the burrow got a reason for the dark and the breather in one - the way out is
  plugged with earth, the child rubs it off (`zdrapka` wyjscie_z_nory, painted for the story instead of the sample
  fogged window: a burrow has no window) and finds the rooster; "catch the fireflies" (the overused breather) is gone -
  the night at the oak is the Great Bear joined from the smallest star; the sunrise is an event illustration; the day's
  method on a plain-colour insert; the four cards in the story became Squirrel's drawing of Mole's day (the story strip
  `dzien_kreta`, scattered by a gust), the cards stay for practice; the home stage is a third act (the sleepover, Mole's
  pyjamas in the bath, the insert, the rug and the bedside table as drop places, the morning SEEN in `pobudka_rano`, the
  home task played); every "... Wskaż." made one sentence, the bare "Brawo!" in practice became lines that name the
  answer; the editing pass: dialogues as two-shots, no wide shot cut away at once, instructions said in the task's shot.
