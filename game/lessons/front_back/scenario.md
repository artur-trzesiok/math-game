# In front and behind - hide-and-seek

Goal: in front of / behind - behind a thing you are hidden and only a bit peeks out; in front of it everybody sees you.
Age 3-4. Characters: Hedgehog, Bunny, Bear. Stage 5, lesson 18.

**Misconception:** hiding = not seeing - Bunny stands in front of the bush with his eyes shut: "nobody can see me!". At
home he does it again with his teddy ("he shut his eyes, so nobody sees him").

**The thread:** Bunny wants to hide well once - the forest shows him how (and his ears still give him away); at the end
of the story he invites everybody home: "this time Bear won't find me!". The home stage is that rematch, and he wins it
(seen: Bear pulls the curtain aside).

No counting past three (counting_3 comes before, counting_5 after): Bear counts "one, two, three".

## Stage 1 - story
A. *Forest.* Hide-and-seek; Bear will count to three. **The seeker counting** (event illustration `mis_liczy`): Bear's
face in his paws at a tree, Hedgehog tiptoes to a stump, Bunny stands IN FRONT of a bush with his eyes shut. Back in
the forest Bunny stands in front of the bush: "I shut my eyes, nobody sees me!" - Bear sees him at once; Hedgehog: in
front everybody sees you, even with your eyes shut. Bunny: "then how do I hide?" **Insert** on a plain colour (the
method): the duckling stands in front of the bush - all of her is seen; she walks behind it - the bush covers her, only
her head peeks out. Bunny gets it and runs off to hide. **Child** hides the mouse behind the bush (`place` "za": her
ears peek out). Then the child seeks: a bush, a stump and a stone - Bunny's ears and the duckling's head peek out from
behind two of them; **child** points to each hiding place; the one found jumps out (each answer named: "only your ears
were peeking out"). *Breather* (`ktoPowiedzial`): one more round - Hedgehog and Bunny hide so well that nothing peeks
out, so Bear listens: a voice calls "a kuku!" from behind a bush and the **child** points to that bush.
B. *Meadow, Mouse's little house.* "In front - behind": **child** puts the teddy in front of the house (`place`
"przed", new relation), then behind it; then points to what is in front of the house (a ball, named) and who hides
behind it (a hen).
C. *Meadow, then the garden maze* (`ogrod_labirynt`, the place of its own). The mouse hid again and Bear cannot find
her. Bunny took a photo while everyone hid (`znalezisko` zdjecie_chowany, flies up from Bunny): **child** points to
where the mouse is - only her ears peek out from BEHIND the hedge, while the hen stands IN FRONT of the bench, seen
whole. Bunny knows the hedge: the garden with the maze. Arrival, a look around; Bear is too big for the maze, Hedgehog
is small and walks in through the arch; **child** leads him through the hedge maze (`labirynt` labirynt_chowany) to the
mouse. Found: she was behind the hedge, so nobody saw her. Hedgehog: hide behind something - in front everybody sees
you. Bunny: "come to my house - this time Bear won't find me!"

## Stage 2 - practice (a plain colour)
The mouse behind the box; the duckling in front of the basket; whose ears / comb peek out from behind the bush (the
bunny or the hen - not a character standing on screen); what is in front of the armchair; the bunny behind the big
stone. Every right answer is named ("Tak, kurka! Wystawał jej czerwony grzebyk.") with the answer in the shot; five
stars.

## Stage 3 - at home: the third act (`pokoj`, Bunny's room)
1. **Arrival with the thread**: Bunny welcomes everybody - the rematch, "now I know how to hide".
2. **The misconception again**: Bunny stands his teddy in front of the armchair: "he shut his eyes, nobody sees him!";
   Hedgehog: "again? in front of the armchair everybody sees him"; Bunny laughs at himself: "the same as in the forest!".
3. **Interactions, all different**: **child** hides the teddy behind the armchair (`place`, his ears peek out); Bear
   counts to three, Bunny slips behind the curtain (his feet peek out under it), Bear looks and looks - **child** points
   where Bunny hides among the curtain, the armchair (the teddy's ears - not him) and the box (`task`); tidying up,
   **child** puts the ball and the block on the painted rug in front of the armchair, where everybody sees them
   (`sort` into the room's drop place `dywan`); Bunny drew the day for his mum and the pictures got mixed up: **child** puts them in order
   (`historyjka` dzien_chowanego: in front of the bush, behind the bush, the mouse found in the maze).
4. **The payoff SEEN**: the event illustration `zajac_za_zaslona` - Bear pulls the curtain aside and Bunny laughs
   behind it, the teddy's ears peek out from behind the armchair: Bunny hid well at last.
5. **The home task shown in play**: Hedgehog hides the mouse behind the box and puts the ball in front; Bunny guesses
   ("behind the box - I see her ears; the ball is in front"); then the line for the parents.

The curtain and the armchair stay stickers in the painted room: "behind" needs the hidden thing drawn UNDER its cover,
which a flat painted plate cannot do (a drop place is a surface things stand, hang or lie ON). The painted rug is a
real drop place: the front of the rug is in front of the armchair (the bookcase shelves lie outside the 4:3 safe area).
