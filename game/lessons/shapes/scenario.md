# Shapes - a house for Hedgehog

Goal: circle, triangle, square, rectangle - told by corners and sides, not by colour or turn. Age 3-4. Characters:
Hedgehog, Fox, Owl, Bunny. Stage 2, lesson 7. World 1 (the forest land): the meadow, the playground, the kitchen.

**Misconception:** a square turned on its corner "is not a square any more" - it is pointy, so it must be another
shape. Said by Fox at the opening, by Bunny about his kite, by Bunny again at home about a biscuit. The answer is always
the same words: we know a shape by its corners and sides; a turned square is still a square.

One thread: **Hedgehog has no house for the winter** - the story builds it from shapes, the kite flies again over the
playground, at home the friends bake shape biscuits for Hedgehog's first tea in his new house, and the tea is seen.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. The meadow - a pointy square (the concept comes in)
1. Fox: who is hiding behind the bush? Shy Hedgehog: winter is coming and he has no house. Owl: we'll build you one!
   Fox: from what? Owl: from shapes - they lie in the grass (a square, a circle, a triangle, a rectangle and a second
   square standing on its corner). For the walls we need a square.
2. **Fox's mistake:** "That one is the square. This one is not - it stands on its corner, it is pointy, it's some other
   shape!" Owl (two-shot): turn it then - the pointy one turns and is the same as the square. Fox, puzzled: then how do
   you know what shape a thing is? Owl: by its corners and sides, not by how it stands. "What does that mean?" - "I'll
   show you: we'll draw them with a finger."
3. **Insert on a plain colour (the method):** **child** traces a circle (no corners at all), a triangle (counts its
   three corners by touch), a square (touches its corners; all sides the same).
4. Back on the meadow, the shapes in the grass again (the pointy square turned once more): **child** points to ALL the
   squares. Fox, the problem solved: both have four corners and equal sides - a turned square is still a square. Now
   we know the shapes - we build!

### B. The meadow - Hedgehog's drawing, torn
5. Hedgehog shows the drawing of the house he dreams of. Bunny hops in, "show me, show me!", grabs it - it tears
   (`podarty`). Bunny, sorry. Owl: never mind, let's put it together. **Child** puts the torn drawing together.
6. To build the same house we look at which shapes it is made of: **child** points to the triangle on the drawing - the
   roof; Owl names the rest (square walls, round window, rectangle door).
7. The pieces lie in the grass (a square, a circle, a rectangle and one tiny triangle). Each place drawn in pencil;
   **child** drags the square onto the walls.
8. The roof: Bunny finds the tiny triangle - too small for a roof. Owl: then let's make a big one - three corners
   joined make a triangle. **Child** joins the three dots from the smallest (`kropki` by size, `dach_trojkat`) - a
   painted roof appears; the big triangle flies onto the walls.
9. **Child** drags the circle onto the window and the rectangle onto the door. The house is ready - just like the
   drawing; Hedgehog walks in, a heart, thank you.
10. A gust - Bunny's kite, left in the grass, is blown away towards the playground. Fox: let's go and find it!

### C. The playground - Bunny's kite (helping, and the breather)
11. Four kites caught at the playground. Bunny (close-up, sad): none of them is mine - mine was a square, and these
    are not: that one stands on its corner! (the misconception again). Fox: remember - corners and sides. **Child**
    points to the square kite. The geometric square laid on it, it turns round and round: four corners, all sides equal -
    a turned square is still a square. Bunny: it's mine!
12. *Breather, no maths (`latawiec`):* Bunny's kite flies again - **child** pulls the string up until it loops.
13. Hedgehog invites everyone to tea in his new house tomorrow; Fox: we'll bake biscuits for it. Home.

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert, drifting shapes, five stars)
1. Tangram: the little house from pieces - named when done ("the house from pieces, like Hedgehog's").
2. The triangle among turned shapes - "yes, three corners, even when it is turned".
3. Trace a rectangle - "two long sides and two short ones".
4. The shape with no corners - "yes, the circle".
5. Colour the house by SIGNS (not digits: numbers come later).

## Stage 3 - at home - the third act (the kitchen, painted places)
14. Arrival with the thread (two-shot): Hedgehog's tea is tomorrow; Fox: shape biscuits, like your house! Biscuits of
    the four shapes on the tray, four plates on the painted table (`miejsca`: `stol`).
15. One rectangle biscuit is missing for Owl: **child** cuts it out of the dough by tracing a rectangle (`slad`); the
    biscuit appears.
16. **Bunny's mistake again:** "each shape on its own plate - I'll help!" He puts a triangle biscuit on a plate, then
    the square biscuit lying on its corner with it: "it's pointy, so it's a triangle!" Hedgehog: look at its corners -
    **child** touches the four corners of the square laid over it (no numbers). Hedgehog: four corners, equal sides -
    a square, only turned. Bunny: a turned square is still a square!
17. **Child** puts all the biscuits on the plates, each plate one shape (`sort`, any order).
18. While they cool - the home game shown in play, "I spy a shape": Fox spies something round, Hedgehog finds the clock
    on the wall; Hedgehog spies something with three corners - **child** points to it among the things on the painted
    shelf and counter (a plate, a chocolate bar, a triangle sandwich).
19. The payoff, SEEN: **event illustration** `herbatka_u_jeza` - the tea at Hedgehog's shape house, the biscuits on four
    plates, the kite flying from the roof. Fox: play "I spy a shape" at home with your parents!

## Iterations
- v1 was a lecture over Owl's birdhouse with quiz rounds. v2 built Hedgehog's house: the shapes traced, the drawing,
  the house, Bunny's turned kite; the catching breather was dropped by an agent; home was one kitchen question.
- v3 (2026-10-29, the improvement plan): a misconception scene at the opening (Fox: the pointy square is "another
  shape"), answered by "corners and sides", the tracings moved onto a plain-colour insert as the method; the drawing is
  torn by Bunny and put together (`podarty`); the roof made by joining three dots (`kropki`, by size); Bunny's kite is
  his own misconception again and flies afterwards (the `latawiec` breather); the home stage is a third act in the
  painted kitchen (Hedgehog's tea: a biscuit traced, Bunny's turned biscuit, corners, the plates, "I spy", the tea seen);
  practice names every right answer; every "Wskaż." became one sentence.
