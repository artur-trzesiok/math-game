# Stage 1 - the plan (one and many, finding differences, bigger and smaller, long and short)

The four lessons of the flower stage, planned on guidelines.md (a story that leads, one thread over the three stages,
a naive try and its visible consequence, a helping adventure, a breather without maths, five different practice
games, the home game) and on the engine's tools. Each lesson has its own places, cast and kind of story, so the
stage never repeats itself. Target length: about 10 minutes a lesson (the rhythm lesson's story alone is 4+ minutes
of speech - too long for 3-4 year olds).

| | idea | thread | places | cast | kind of story |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | one / many | Squirrel's winter store | garden, brook (ducks), kitchen | Squirrel, Deer, Fox | harvest and a lost duckling |
| 2 | spot the difference | the magic pond | glade with a pond, meadow, room | Owl, Fox | a playful riddle (a trickster pond) |
| 3 | bigger / smaller | a new home for Squirrel | Squirrel's tree, forest path, her new room | Squirrel, Fox, Owl | "too small - too big - just right" |
| 4 | long / short | a bridge for the picnic | brook, meadow, hallway | Deer, Fox, Squirrel | building and a race |

## 1. One or many?

**Idea:** one thing vs many things ("jeden" / "wiele"), what there is one of and what there are many of. Not yet
numbers. **Misconception:** big means many - "I brought a lot!" with one huge pumpkin.

- **Story.** Autumn: Squirrel gathers her store for the winter and her friends help.
  1. *Garden.* Fox rolls in one enormous pumpkin, proud: "a lot of food!". Squirrel (dismayed): "it is one
     pumpkin - big, but one; many is when there are lots of things", and shows a basket of many nuts. The concept
     named: one / many. **Child** points: where is one, where are many (pumpkin / nuts, a sun / clouds).
  2. Gathering: carrots, apples, one cabbage... **child** drags each into the "one" plate or the "many" basket
     (sorting zones).
  3. *Brook - helping.* Mother Duck is upset: her many ducklings went swimming, one is missing. The ducklings swim
     past in a group; **child** finds the one alone behind the reeds (a pointing task), it swims back to the many.
  4. *Breather:* the ducklings' race - point quickly to the ones that pop up (catching game, no maths).
  5. Back home with the store: many nuts in the hollow - and one pumpkin for a pie, and everybody laughs.
- **Practice** (an insert, five stars): one clap or many claps? (counting by ear, `dzwieki`); under which cup is one
  ball and under which many (`kubki`); memory pairs "one with one, many with many" (`memory`); one tree, many
  leaves - point to the one; the shadow of one apple or of a bunch (`cien`).
- **Home (kitchen):** laying the table - one cake for everybody, many spoons; the child gives one plate to Fox
  and many cherries into a bowl. "Look around with your parents: what do you have one of, what many of?"

## 2. Spot the difference

**Idea:** look carefully at two pictures and find what is not the same (missing, extra, another colour, another
size). **Misconception:** a quick look - "they are the same!".

- **Story.** The magic pond on the glade shows things a little differently.
  1. *The pond.* Fox looks into the water - his reflection has no scarf! Owl: the pond plays tricks, it changes
     things in its pictures; let's find them. Fox and his reflection side by side (`odbicie`, see tools): **child**
     points to what is different (the scarf, an ear tuft, the colour of a flower beside him).
  2. The pond's game: two pictures of the meadow side by side, three differences (`roznice`, see tools); a found one
     is circled in both pictures.
  3. *Helping.* Owl's two drawings for her granny got mixed - one is the copy the wind smudged; find the differences
     to know which is the real one.
  4. *Breather:* hide and seek - Fox hides, the child closes the eyes (the screen dims), point to where he is.
  5. The pond, satisfied, shows a last picture - exactly the same: "no differences - it is just a mirror now".
- **Practice:** three picture pairs (`roznice`), one "what is missing" (a sticker taken off a row), one "which one is
  not like the others" (a shadow, `cien`).
- **Home (Fox's room):** the child looks at the room, closes the eyes, Squirrel moves one toy; what changed? Twice.
  "Play it at home: one closes the eyes, the other moves one thing."

## 3. Bigger and smaller (rethought)

**Why rethink:** the present story is four unconnected things (rain and wellies, rock-paper-scissors at the brook,
Owl's hat, a present of hats at home - hats twice), and it compares mostly things handed to the characters, not a
problem they have. **New idea:** one thread with a visible consequence at every step - Goldilocks: too small,
too big, just right. Keeps the overlapping method (`nalozenie`) and adds the line of comparison.
**Misconception:** "the higher it hangs, the bigger it is" / "my old house, it is fine" (it is too small now).

- **Story.** Squirrel's hollow has become too small (her store did not fit - continues lesson 1).
  1. *Squirrel's tree.* She squeezes in - only her head goes in, her tail sticks out (a visible consequence, a
     squash animation). Fox: we will find you a bigger hollow. Concept: bigger / smaller, and how to check: lay one
     thing on the other (her old door on a new one).
  2. *Forest path (pan).* Three hollows in three trees: a tiny one (she does not get in), a huge one (the wind blows
     her hat off - too big, cold), a right one. **Child** points to the bigger / smaller each time, then checks by
     overlapping the doors.
  3. *Helping.* A mouse family moving in after her: their box is too small for all of them - give the bigger box
     (choosing what fits).
  4. *Breather:* a quick catching game - acorns fall, catch the big ones for the store.
  5. Squirrel in her new hollow, everything fits; she invites everybody to furnish it.
- **Practice:** which is bigger (pair, then the overlap shows it); the biggest of three; shadows - which shadow is
  the big apple's (`cien`); hats onto heads by size (`ubieranie`); a catching game.
- **Home (the new room):** a big bed and a small one, a big cup and a small one - who gets which (by the
  characters' real sizes). "Compare the family's shoes with your parents: lay one on the other."

## 4. Long and short

**Idea:** long / short, longer / shorter; the method - start at the same line. **Misconception:** the stick lying
further along looks longer - it was only pushed ahead.

- **Story.** The friends go for a picnic across the brook.
  1. *Brook.* No bridge. Planks lie on the bank; Squirrel picks one that "looks long" because it lies far out - too
     short, it falls into the water (a splash, a consequence). Deer: put them side by side, ends on one line, then
     see which is longer. **Child** drags planks to the start line, points to the long one; it reaches across.
  2. Crossing (`przejdz` over the plank).
  3. *Helping.* Deer's scarf is too short for her long neck and she is cold; choose the long one of two scarves
     (dragged on - she is a quadruped, so it is laid around her neck as a sticker... see tools).
  4. *Breather:* a race of two snails on the meadow - cheer, no maths.
  5. Picnic: a long baguette and a short one - the long one for everybody.
- **Practice:** which is longer (two ribbons, then aligned at the line); the shortest of three; a snake longer
  than the other; a long and a short train (memory pairs?); trace a long and a short line (`slad`).
- **Home (hallway):** scarves on the hooks - the long for Deer, the short for Squirrel; shoelaces. "Compare
  shoelaces or spaghetti with your parents - from one line."

## What is needed

Already in the engine: sorting zones, tasks, `nalozenie`, `ubieranie`, `przejdz`, `pan`, `ujecie`, `emocja`,
`czastki`, `kubki`, `memory`, `dzwieki`, `cien`, `slad`, the catching game of bigger_smaller_pro.

**New tools (for acceptance):**
1. `roznice` - picture pairs for "spot the difference": gpt-image draws a picture, then an edit "the same picture
   with three things changed"; the code finds the changed areas by comparing the two (a pipeline like the
   backgrounds' split) and the game makes them targets in both pictures, circling a found one in both.
2. `odbicie` - a character's reflection in water: the puppet drawn again upside down under the bank with a water
   ripple, and able to show differences (a garment off, a colour) - lesson 2's opening.
3. A garment for a quadruped laid on as a sticker (a scarf around Deer's neck) - today quadrupeds are never dressed.
   Or the scarf goes to Squirrel and lesson 4 avoids it.

**New pictures (the usual pipelines):** ducklings and a mother duck, a cabbage, cherries, three tree hollows (or
doors), a mouse family, beds big and small, planks long and short, ribbons, baguettes, snails, scarves long and
short.
