# Long and short

Goal: long / short, longer / shorter; the method - things compared from ONE line. Age 3-4. Characters: Deer (right),
Fox and Squirrel (left). Stage 1, lesson 4. Thread: **a picnic across the brook** - the story gets them there (a
plank bridge, a scarf against the wind, the shorter path on Deer's map), the picnic is seen; at home Squirrel gives
Fox back the long scarf he lent her, and dinner is pasta, the longest for Fox, the shortest for Squirrel.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. The brook - the plank that "reaches further"
1. The three walk in to the brook (wide). Deer: a lovely day, a picnic on the glade across the brook. Fox (medium,
   pointing): the bridge is far away - let's make our own, from a plank.
2. Two planks on the grass, the short one lying further out. Deer (medium on the planks): we need a long one. Squirrel
   (medium, pointing): this one is longer, it reaches further! (the misconception)
3. Wide: the short plank across the water - it does not reach and falls in. Squirrel (close-up, sad): too short! Fox
   fishes it out: but how do we know which one is longer?
4. **Insert on a plain colour (the method):** the planks lie anyhow; Deer: that one only lay further out; to compare
   length, lay them from ONE line. **Child** drags both planks to the pencil line; the ends get ticks; **child** points
   to the longer. Squirrel (close-up): now I see it.
5. Back at the brook: the long plank laid across reaches the far bank (medium); the three walk over it into the
   picture; Deer: we are across!

### B. The meadow - the wind, the snails and the map
6. The wind. Squirrel (close-up, cold): my scarf is too short. Fox's second scarf: **child** lays both from the line
   and points to the longer; Squirrel puts it on (close-up, heart): it goes all round, I'm warm.
7. *Breather, no maths:* the snail race - point to a snail to cheer it on; at the flower Fox cheers.
8. Deer's map (`znalezisko` mapa_pikniku): two paths from the same start on the meadow to the blanket. Fox is hungry -
   **child** points to the shorter path; Deer names both (this one almost straight, that one round the hill).
9. **Walk on the map** (`wedrowka` mapa_pikniku, dobra 0): **child** leads Fox's pawn along the short path to the
   blanket; stepping onto the long path the pawn shakes and Fox says why (round the hill - we'd be late).

### C. The picnic glade - the arrival is a beat of its own (`polana_piknik`)
10. Arrival (a transition, the three walk in, wide): Squirrel looks round - here already, the short path was quick;
    the blanket, the basket.
11. Fox (close-up): my tummy is rumbling - what's in the basket? Deer: two baguettes, laid from one line on the
    blanket; we need the one that will do for all three of us - **child** points to the longer.
12. The payoff seen - **event illustration** `piknik_polana`: the three on the blanket, Fox breaking the long baguette,
    the short path behind them. Deer: everybody got a piece. Squirrel: this evening, home - and Fox gets his scarf back.

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert with clouds, five stars)
Ribbons every which way - from the line, then the longer; the shortest of three planks; two pencil snakes; shadows of a
long or a short scarf; catch the long ribbon (twice). Shots: the task wide, the right answer named in a medium shot.

## Stage 3 - at home - the third act
13. Arrival with the thread (the hallway `przedpokoj`): Squirrel comes in wearing Fox's scarf (two-shot): thank you
    for the long scarf! The two scarves come off and lie on the floor.
14. **Child** hangs both scarves on the painted hook rail (`miejsca` wieszak): they hang from one rail - Deer: like from
    one line, so now you can see which is longer. **Child** points to Fox's scarf - the longer one. Fox: mine, thanks.
15. The home problem with the misconception again (the kitchen `dom`): dinner - pasta lies anyhow on the table; Fox
    grabs the one whose end sticks out furthest: "this one is the longest!" Deer: it only lies further out, like the
    plank. **Child** lays all five from the pencil line - Fox's pasta is short (the visible consequence; close-up).
16. **Child** points to the longest pasta (Fox's), then the shortest (Squirrel's); both into the pot.
17. The payoff - **event illustration** `obiad_makaron`: Fox slurping one endless spaghetti, Squirrel with one tiny
    elbow macaroni.
18. The home task shown in play: back in the kitchen, Fox and Squirrel each lay a spaghetti from the table's edge line
    - Squirrel's broken one is shorter. Deer: compare shoelaces or spaghetti with your parents - always from one line!

## Iterations
- The naive try is the classic misconception: the end that reaches further is taken for the longer thing; the plank
  falling into the water is its visible consequence, the start line is the method.
- Deer is a quadruped and is never dressed, so the scarf goes to Squirrel (the user's choice).
- Every comparison has the same thing at two lengths (one picture sheet, one scale), never two different things.
- v-next (2026-10-29, the lesson pass): the method is shown on a plain-colour insert; the picnic glade got a beat of
  its own (the arrival, the baguettes from one line, the picnic seen as an event illustration) - it was three lines
  with no interaction; after pointing to the shorter path the child walks it on the map (`wedrowka`); the home stage is
  a third act: Squirrel returns Fox's scarf (the thread), the scarves hang on the painted hooks (from one rail = from
  one line), Fox repeats the misconception with the pasta and sees his pick is short, dinner seen, the home task played
  on screen; every "Wskaż." is one sentence and every right answer is named. Not used: the torn map (three tasks in a
  row on one card would be too much for a 3-year-old; the wet map had no story beat of its own) and a brook-to-meadow
  panorama (the plank bridge is placed on the painted banks of `strumyk`; the crossing is already a walk into the
  picture, a pan would add nothing to it).
