# Stage 4 - the plan (numbers 1 to 5, up and down, inside and outside, tangram)

The tower stage, planned like stages 1-3. Counting grows from three to five and gets its first tool (a ticket that
costs as many coins as its number); then two lessons of position, the first of "where is it" (up / down, in / out);
the stage ends with building pictures from pieces. Each lesson has its own places, cast and kind of story: a journey,
an orchard on a windy day, an evening on a farm, a shadow theatre.

| | idea | thread | places | cast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | numbers 1 to 5 (rethought) | Mole's little forest train | station (new), forest, room | Mole, Bear, Owl |
| 14 | up and down (new) | apple day at Squirrel's tree | orchard, autumn orchard, hallway | Squirrel, Bunny, Fox |

| 16 | tangram (rethought) | Owl's shadow theatre | room at night, theatre (new), kitchen | Owl, Bear, Squirrel |

Stage 3 taught counting to three by touch; here the count goes to five, a number is matched to a quantity both ways
(how many? - give as many as), and a row's length is not its number (conservation).

## 13. Numbers 1 to 5 (rethought)

The old lesson (Squirrel's pine cones) introduces the digits one by one with Owl's rhymes and is all "how many? point
to the digit". Rethought around a journey with something to DO with each number.
**Idea:** count to five, every thing once; the digits 4 and 5 (1-3 are known); a number tells how many to give, not
only how many there are; five spread out is still five.
**Misconception:** a longer row has more - Bear's five apples lie far apart, Owl's five close together: "mine are more!".

- **Story.** Mole opens a little train through the forest; everybody wants to ride to the lake.
  1. *Station.* Mole: a ticket costs as many coins as the number on it. Bear's ticket says 4: **child** pays four
     coins (`sklepik`, a fifth jumps back). The digit 4 drawn, **child** traces it (`slad`).
  2. The wagons have numbers 1-5: **child** seats each passenger in the wagon of their ticket (`slots`).
  3. *Forest, on the way.* Bear and Owl share apples - two rows of five, Bear's spread out: "I have more!". **Child**
     counts both rows (`count`, `rzad`): five and five, the same. The digit 5.
  4. *Breather:* the train whistles - how many toots (`dzwieki`, 3-5), point to the digit.
  5. The lake: five friends got off - count them (characters as targets).
- **Practice:** how many (4-5 things) - point to the digit; give Mole five coins for a ticket; trace the 5; which
  basket has four; jump along the stones to 5 (`os`).
- **Home:** count five fingers on a hand (a sticker hand, fingers bump); "count to five with your parents: stairs,
  spoons, claps".

## 14. Up and down (new)

**Idea:** up / down (at the top, at the bottom, above, below) - up is towards the sky, down towards the ground; the
words for where a thing is on a tree, a shelf, a lift.
**Misconception:** up and down depend on how you are standing - Bunny hangs upside down from a branch and says the
apples are down and the grass is up. Fox: whichever way you hang, up is the sky.

- **Story.** Squirrel's apple day: the apples are ripe, the wind is coming.
  1. *Orchard.* Squirrel up in the tree (an `idea` of the plan), Bunny below. Bunny hangs head down: "the apples are
     down there!" - everybody laughs; Fox: up = sky, down = grass. **Child**: point to what is up (a bird, an apple),
     what is down (a mushroom, a hedgehog's basket).
  2. Squirrel sends apples down and Bunny sends the empty basket up: **child** works the LIFT (a basket on a rope;
     see new mechanics) - up to Squirrel, down to Bunny.
  3. *Autumn orchard, the wind.* Leaves and apples are blown about: **child** sorts what flies up (a balloon, a
     leaf, a bird) and what falls down (an apple, a stone, a pine cone) (`sort`).
  4. *Breather:* "up - down" - Fox says, the characters jump or squat; one does it wrong (`zamroz` is not needed - a
     `taniec` of two moves): who did it wrong.
  5. The apples are in the basket at the bottom, the bird in its nest at the top.
- **Practice:** put the jar on the top shelf / the shoes on the bottom one (`place` on shelves); the lift to the top
  floor and back; what is higher (two birds); memory up-down pairs (a balloon up / a stone down); which one is at
  the very top.
- **Home:** the hallway: hats go up on the hooks, shoes down on the mat (sort). "Play 'up - down' with your parents:
  hands up to the sky, down to the floor."

## 15. Inside and outside (new)

**Idea:** inside / outside (in, out of, in the middle, around) - a closed fence makes an inside; a gap in it lets
things out.
**Misconception:** next to = inside - Beaver puts the duckling next to the basket ("it's in!"); and a fence with a gap
does not keep anyone in.

- **Story.** A storm is coming in the evening; Hedgehog wants everybody inside.
  1. *Meadow.* Hedgehog's hens are out. Beaver builds a fence - but leaves a gap; a hen walks out through it. **Child**
     closes the fence with a finger (the FENCE; see new mechanics) - now the hens are inside.
  2. Beaver puts a duckling next to the basket: "it's in!" - Deer: next to is not inside. **Child** puts the duckling
     in (`place` "w").
  3. *Burrow.* The shell game in the warm burrow: Hedgehog hides an acorn under one of three cups, shuffles -
     which cup is it in? (`kubki`).
  4. *Breather:* the rain starts - who is still outside? **child** points at the characters outside the porch.
  5. Everybody inside; the rain outside; a cup of tea.
- **Practice:** draw a fence around the sheep only (not the wolf); put three things in the box; what is outside the
  circle (pointing); which cup hides the ball; inside or outside: sort pictures (a fish in the bowl, a bird out of
  the cage).
- **Home:** toys back in the toy box (drag into the box, one sits on the lid - "on is not in"). "Play 'in - out' with
  your parents: in the box, out of the box."

## 16. Tangram (rethought)

The old lesson (Squirrel's square puzzle falls apart) teaches the tap-to-turn with a square, house, fish and boat. Kept
as the method, rethought as a theatre: the silhouettes ARE shadows on a sheet.
**Idea:** a picture from pieces - every piece has its place; a piece that does not fit is turned, not thrown away; the
same pieces make different pictures.
**Misconception:** "this piece is wrong" - Bear throws away the triangle that does not fit, it only needed turning.

- **Story.** Owl's shadow theatre tonight; the puppets got mixed up into a pile of shapes.
  1. *Room at night.* Owl: a theatre needs puppets - we make them from these shapes. A house from two pieces; Bear
     says the triangle is wrong and throws it; Owl turns it - it fits. **Child** builds the house (`tangram`).
  2. *Theatre (new scenery: a sheet lit from behind).* Every silhouette appears as a shadow on the sheet - the fish,
     the boat: **child** builds each; when done, the shadow on the sheet comes alive (it swims, sails).
  3. A new puppet from the same pieces: a cat (new silhouette, four pieces).
  4. *Breather:* shadow guessing - Owl shows a sticker's shadow (`cien`), whose shadow is it (three stickers).
  5. The show: the shadows play the story; the audience claps (count the claps is NOT added: one thing at a time).
- **Practice:** a fish; which piece fits the gap (three pieces); a boat with turned pieces; whose shadow; a bird (new
  silhouette).
- **Home:** the kitchen: a square from two triangles, then a house. "Cut a square from paper with your parents and
  make pictures from its pieces."

## New things this stage needs

Mechanics (to agree first):
- `winda` - a lift: a basket on a rope over a branch/pulley; the child pulls the rope (drag down = basket up) and the
  basket stops at levels; things ride in it. Used in 14; later floors 1-5 of a house (counting) and "higher / lower".
- `zagroda` - a fence drawn with a finger: a closed line around things; what is inside the loop is inside (point in
  polygon), a line left open has a gap and things walk out. Used in 15; later grouping ("draw a fence around all the
  red ones").

Without new mechanics: tickets (`sklepik`), wagons (`slots`), rows (`count`), toots (`dzwieki`), stones (`os`), shelves
(`place`), sorting (`sort`), the shell game (`kubki`), shadows (`cien`), tangram silhouettes (new data: kot, ptak).

Scenery: `stacyjka` (a little forest railway station with a train), `teatrzyk` (a shadow theatre: a white sheet lit
from behind, a little stage). Stickers: tickets, coins, wagons, a hand, a balloon, a pine cone, a basket on a rope,
hats and shoes, hens, a duckling, a toy box, sheep and a wolf, a fishbowl, a cage...
