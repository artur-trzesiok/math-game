# Will it fit? (thick_wide)

Goal: thick / thin is said about round things (a barrel, logs, trunks, crayons, books), wide / narrow about flat things
and openings (a gate, a tunnel, ribbons, scarves, a picture); whether a thing goes through a gap is decided by how thick
or wide it is, never by how long; three things can be put in order from the thinnest. Age 3-4. Characters: Mole (left,
the one moving house, sees badly), Fox (right, the misconception), Deer (right, the calm helper). Stage 6, after
counting_5 and light_heavy: the child knows counting and digits to 5, bigger / smaller, long / short, tall / short and
heavy / light; thick and wide are new.

**Misconception:** longer = thicker / wider - Fox: "the barrel is short, it will go through; the long stick won't".

One thread: **Mole moves into a new burrow, and Fox and Deer help carry his things** - through the narrow garden gate,
between the birches, down the tunnels; the burrow is under the thickest tree in the wood. The story ends inside the new
burrow with Mole's invitation to his housewarming party; at home (the new burrow's hallway) the gifts are put away
before the guests come in.

## Stage 1 - story

### A. The garden gate (`ogrod`) - the barrel sticks, the long stick passes (the concept comes in)
1. Mole (two-shot with Deer): "Hooray! Today I move house - my new burrow is in the birch wood." Deer: we'll help carry
   your things! Mole's barrel and his long stick float over the heads; Mole: we take the barrel and the stick.
2. The narrow garden gate (`szpara`, `szpara_furtka`, first use) stands in the middle. Mole (medium on Mole and the
   gate): first we must get through this gate. Fox (medium on Fox and both things): "The barrel is short, it will
   surely go through! And the stick is long - that one won't." Deer: let's check - **child** pushes the barrel through
   the gate: it sticks out over the posts and wobbles (Fox, the wrong line: "Oh! The barrel is stuck!").
3. Fox (close-up, "?"): but the barrel is short! Deer: now try the stick. **Child** pushes the long stick: it slides in
   behind the posts and goes through. Fox (close-up, "!"): "The long stick went through!"
4. **Insert (plain colour):** the gate alone, the barrel and the stick beside it; a pencil line across the gate's
   opening and one across the barrel. Deer: the gate is narrow - only something thin goes through. The stick is long
   but thin, so it went through; the barrel is short but thick. Fox (close-up): "Aha! Long doesn't mean thick."
5. Back in the garden: the narrow gate and, next to it, a wide gateway in a stone wall (`szpara_brama`). Mole (two-shot
   with Deer): how will we carry the thick barrel? Deer: through the wide gateway! **Child** pushes the barrel through
   the gate of his choice: the narrow one keeps it out ("too narrow"), the wide one lets it through. Fox names it: the
   thick barrel went through the wide gateway. Mole: everything is through - to the wood!

### B. The birch wood (`las`) - between two birches, the thickest tree
6. Walk in (wide). Mole: here is the birch wood, my burrow is close. Three upright logs for his stove float over the
   heads (thick, middle, thin, the same height); the path runs between two birches standing close (`szpara_drzewa`).
   Fox (medium on Fox and the logs): "I'll take the biggest log!" Deer: between the birches it's narrow - **child**
   pushes through the log that fits: the thick ones stick ("too thick"), the thinnest one passes. Mole names it: the
   thinnest log went between the birches; Fox carries the thick ones round.
7. Mole (close-up, worried): my burrow is under the thickest tree in the wood - but I see so badly... Three trees of
   one height with trunks thick, middle and thin stand on the ground. Deer: we'll help - **child** points to the
   thickest tree. Mole (rays behind the tree): "Yes, this tree has the thickest trunk - I live here!"

### C. At the new burrow - the doormat, two tunnels, everything inside
8. *Breather, no maths* (`zdrapka` `wycieraczka_kreta`): Mole: "Oh no, the doormat by my door is all muddy!" Fox:
   let's rub the mud off - there's something under it. **Child** rubs the mud away: a little mole and flowers are
   painted on the mat. Mole: "My mole on the doormat!" **Child** points to the painted mole.
9. Two holes lead into the burrow on the ground (stickers `tunel_waski`, `tunel_szeroki`). Mole (medium on Mole and the
   holes): only thin things go down the narrow tunnel; thick things go through the wide one. Mole's things wait over
   the heads: the long stick, a thin crayon, the thin log and the barrel, the thick log, a thick crayon. Deer: put the
   thin things into the narrow tunnel and the thick ones into the wide one. **Child** sorts (`sort`, any order). Mole
   names it.
10. **Event illustration** `przeprowadzka_kreta`: inside the cosy burrow everything has found its place - the barrel in
    the corner, the logs by the stove, the stick by the bed; Mole, Fox and Deer unpack.

### D. Inside the new burrow (`nora`, the story's own place)
11. Arrival (wide): Mole - welcome to my new burrow! Deer: how cosy! Mole (close-up): thank you, without you nothing
    would have fitted. Mole (two-shot with Fox): tomorrow is my housewarming party - you're invited! Fox: we'll bring
    presents! (the thread to the home stage)

## Stage 2 - practice (an insert on a soft green, drifting circles, five stars)
1. **Thicker, thinner:** two crayons - point to the thicker one; two trees - point to the one with the thinner trunk.
2. **Wider, narrower:** two ribbons of one length - point to the wider one; laid again - point to the narrower one.
3. **Does it fit?** A narrow gap in a stone wall (`szpara_brama`, narrow), a pumpkin and a little stick over the heads:
   push through what fits - the pumpkin sticks, the thin stick passes.
4. **In order:** three crayons from the thinnest to the thickest (places over the heads, an arrow at the first).
5. **Thick or wide?** A thick crayon and a wide ribbon: point to what is thick; then to what is wide. Fox names the
   words: a crayon is thick, a ribbon is wide.

## Stage 3 - at home - the third act (the new burrow's hallway, `przedpokoj`, its painted shelf and hooks)
12. Arrival with the thread (wide): the housewarming today, the guests are coming; the stand, the umbrella and a thick
    book appear - Deer: we brought so many things, let's tidy up first.
13. The home problem with the same misconception: a long thin umbrella and a narrow umbrella stand. Deer: "This
    umbrella is so long - it won't fit into the narrow stand!" Fox (close-up, "?"): long? remember the stick and the
    gate! **Child** puts into the stand what fits - the umbrella or the thick book (the book is refused: the stand is
    narrow). Deer names it: the umbrella is thin, so it fits.
14. The books onto the painted shelf: **child** stands three books in order from the thinnest to the thickest (places on
    the shelf, an arrow at the first). Mole names it.
15. Three scarves hang on the hooks (one length, three widths). Fox: my neck is cold - **child** points to the widest
    scarf; Fox takes it: the widest one is the warmest.
16. A knock - the guests! Three presents wait by the door; Mole: one of them needs the door opened wide. **Child**
    points to the widest present (the big picture); Mole names it.
17. The payoff, **event illustration** `parapetowka_kreta`: the door wide open, Hedgehog and Squirrel bring the big wide
    picture in, the hallway tidy (the umbrella in its stand, the books in order, the scarves on the hooks).
18. The home game shown (insert on a plain colour): Mole holds a thick crayon, Fox a thin one; Fox says which is which.
    Mole (close-up) to the parents: find with your parents something thicker than your finger and something thinner.

## Iterations
- v1 (2026-10-30), from the plan in etap6_plan.md ("New lessons", 23). Changes from the plan: the barrel and the stick
  are pushed through the gate one after the other (two `szpara` rounds on the same gate) so the barrel is always tried
  first and Fox's bet is tested in its order; the logs and trees in the wood stand upright, so a log's thickness is its
  width the gap measures; the "round tunnel" became two holes, a narrow and a wide one, so the sort has a place for
  the thick things too; at home the wide picture is the present that needs the door opened wide (one task instead of
  the picture on the hook plus the door), and the shelf holds the books.
  Title renamed by the user to "Czy się zmieści?" / "Will it fit?" (the id stays thick_wide): the question is what
  decides whether a thing fits - thickness and width, not length; the pairs stay gruby / cienki and szeroki / wąski.
  Built details: every gap is shown (a plain sticker at the exact place and size `szpara` will draw it) before the
  bet and the instruction are said, and `szpara` is started only after them - started earlier, the child (and the
  autopilot) could push the barrel before Fox's bet; the first round, where the barrel only sticks, runs with the
  lesson's confetti switched off.
